# Ash & Echo — first depth-pass critique

Scope: independent environment composition review; no product edits. Inspected the two supplied reference screenshots, the 390 × 844 native baseline captured from port 8319 as `review-baseline-native.png`, and the candidate opening/jump/landing frames. The opening frame was refreshed during review; its clearer arch moulding is an improvement over the first candidate. This is a bounded still/sequence judgment, not a runtime or full-game quality verdict.

The user's C− is the quality anchor. The oversized arch gives this pass a real architectural scale relationship that the baseline lacked. It does not yet establish reference-level depth. The candidate still mostly reads as sharp platform cutouts in front of a softened scenic plate. The arch is worthwhile; the broad wash of light and weak overlap relationships keep it from feeling like a chamber with measurable distance.

The style is painted, nearly monochrome Gothic architecture in a fixed-width portrait platformer. The visual job is to make the climb feel suspended in an enormous hollow while keeping the next landing and hazard immediate. The reference's useful lesson is the relationship between masses: substantial dark foreground, a distinct intermediate structure, and smaller occluded forms in the light. Its blur alone is not the explanation for its depth.

## Highest-impact corrections

1. **High — the arch aperture contains too little legible distance.** In the initial frames, the right/lower opening is mostly a pale, nearly uniform field; the faint remaining spires do not provide a strong size comparison. Preserve one recognisable distant spire behind the arch's right-hand opening, with its upper contour visible and its base lost in local mist. Shift or narrow the main light so its brightest part occupies an opening between structures, rather than washing the whole lower chamber. A visible overlap at the arch edge and a smaller architectural unit will establish depth more effectively than another diffuse layer. Keep the landing path clear by preserving low contrast in that distant structure.

2. **High — the middle plane needs a coherent mass and distance.** The initial arch loses almost all internal plane changes, while an isolated rounded dark form at the upper right reads as a disconnected cutout. The later, sharper opening frame starts to fix the arch: preserve its broad moulding ridges, underside, and broken crown, without bringing back foreground-level masonry noise. Move the upper-right fragment so its connecting pier is understandable, or crop it completely outside this chamber's aperture. One believable huge architectural span is stronger than two ambiguous overlapping fragments. Acceptance: at native size, a glance should distinguish the arch's thickness from the distant spire, without any stone texture competing with a platform cap.

3. **High — the near plane has no clear silhouette independent of the side wall.** Restricting it to a narrow, mostly black outer strip makes it resemble wall shading. Give one near stone mass a visible irregular diagonal contour across the textured wall interior while keeping it behind the contact edge and clear of the jump route. That contour should hide part of a wall feature, so the occlusion explains which object is nearer. Keep this sparse and asymmetric. A darker strip plus a different scroll rate is insufficient if its boundary cannot be seen. After that, slightly quiet the corbel undersides' fine contrast while preserving their crisp landing caps; the current universal microdetail makes every traversable prop demand equal attention.

## What this material establishes

The opening → jump → landing frames keep the small hero, spike hazard, and first landing readable. The larger arch improves scale and stops the backdrop from being only a repeated distant skyline. These frames do not establish a convincing parallax reveal because the camera barely changes during the first jump. The next useful visual check is a short ordinary climb that carries the camera past the arch, showing the near contour, middle span, and distant spire uncovering one another. Root owns that check and runtime validation.

No A/S or full-game upgrade is warranted by adding layers. The current direction is worth continuing because the main arch changes the composition; the next gain comes from mass placement, value separation, and visible occlusion.
