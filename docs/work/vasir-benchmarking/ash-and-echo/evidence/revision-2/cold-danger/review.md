## What the normal game taught me

Before acting, I identified stone ledge tops as safe, red teeth as dangerous, and distant spires/birds/cages/carved wall creatures as decorative. The cage interpretation retained some uncertainty. The visible instructions invited ascending by movement and jumps.

I walked right into the floor teeth; a burst and ring appeared at the teeth, the game displayed `THORNS — jump clear or kick away.`, and the character returned to the start. I wrote down the cause and intended correction before trying again. I then jumped onto the central ledge and avoided the floor thorns. Screenshots 03–06 document the encounter and changed attempt.

I climbed naturally to the right ledge with the HUD at 118 m. Before contact, I identified the nearby red wall strip as the same danger and predicted that a rightward jump would hit it. That jump caused a burst on the wall strip and a return to the bottom. Before replay, I wrote down the correction: jump left across the open center from that ledge. I climbed back by the learned path and landed on the central ledge safely, with the HUD at 119 m. Screenshots 14–18 document this second loop.

These passes rest on predicted danger, locally visible cause, an understandable reset, and a demonstrated changed attempt—not merely on collision correctness.

## Art and clarity

The original equal triangular peaks and clean pale outlines communicated danger clearly, but their flat geometric repetition looked pasted onto unusually rich chipped stone architecture. The original circle/slash contact effect resembled a prohibition annotation. Both were specific visual weaknesses, even though the danger remained understandable.

After refresh, the revised hooked and chipped-looking thorns retained their red edge and contrast from gray safe surfaces. Their crooked silhouettes fit the ruined architecture better. The revised broken slash marks the contact point as a rupture, without the previous prohibition-circle reading. At 390 × 844 the thorns remain a repeated, stylized motif, but the most obvious geometry-placeholder impression is resolved. Screenshots 20, 23, and 24 show the revised material and contact effect.

I repeated actual floor contact and correction after refresh, then hit the revised left-wall thorns at the ledge previously reached at 58 m. I climbed back and jumped right away from that wall safely, ending on the right ledge with the HUD at 81 m. Screenshots 21–25 and the revised recording preserve this check.

One remaining wording uncertainty: `jump clear` gives an executable correction, but `kick away` names an action whose control was not explained by the visible opening instructions. This did not block either successful correction.
