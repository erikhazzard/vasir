# Ash & Echo implementation seam

Self-contained static ES modules. World width is 420 logical units; y increases downward. Camera uses `game.cameraY` as the world-space top of the viewport. Runtime viewport height is supplied by `game.resize(logicalHeight)`.

`game.js` exports `createGame()`. Returned game exposes `width`, `height`, `viewportHeight`, `player` (`x,y,w,h,vx,vy,grounded,wallDir,airJumps,facing,motion`), `platforms` (`id,x,y,w,h,kind`, with optional `suspension`, `solid`, `route`, `landmark`, `structure`, `support` and `resonance`), `hazards` (`x,y,w,h,orientation`), `checkpoint` (`x,y,w,h,active`), `goal` (`x,y,w,h`), `cameraY`, `time`, `elapsed`, `deaths`, `maxAltitude`, `respawnTimer`, `state` (`ready`,`playing`,`won`), and `events`.

Methods: `resize(height)`, `start()`, `restart()`, `step(dt, input)`, `drainEvents()`. Input: `{axis:-1..1,jumpPressed:boolean,jumpHeld:boolean}`. `jumpPressed` is one press edge, consumed once. Events: `{type,x,y,vx,vy,intensity}`; types `jump`, `doubleJump`, `wallJump`, `land`, `death`, `respawn`, `checkpoint`, `win`. A `land` event additionally identifies `platformId`, `suspended` (boolean), optional `landmark`, normalized `contactOffset`, `fallDistance` (logical feet-to-surface distance from the latest apex), `impactSpeed` (positive vertical speed before collision stops the player), `landingSeverity` (continuous 0–1 presentation weight), and optional `resonance` pivot metadata. Ground `jump` events carry the departing platform’s landmark when present. Death additionally records `cause` (`thorns` or `fall`), `hazardIndex` (-1 without a hazard), and the world-space `contactX,contactY` for causal feedback. `step` uses seconds; main passes fixed 1/120 steps. Larger calls subdivide into steps no larger than 1/120; zero time does nothing. World collisions and level belong to game.js.

Free-hanging ledges have `suspension: {homeX,homeY,x,y,vx,vy}`; its `x,y` are offsets from the authored home position. Side masonry, checkpoint and summit contacts remain fixed. Simulation advances each spring before player movement and writes the live collider coordinates to `platform.x,y`. Offset bounds are ±3.5 horizontally and −4 to +11 vertically, with a loaded vertical equilibrium of +4.2. A grounded rider inherits the supporting ledge's displacement, keeping feet on its live surface; spring velocity does not alter the authored jump impulse. Landing severity scales the stone impulse; takeoff releases it without repeated landing events while it settles. Leaving support releases the rider through the normal coyote/air-jump rules. Death clears grounded contact; springs settle during the retry delay. Restart clears support and spring state and restores every home position.

Landing severity continues increasing with descent distance after the 900-unit/s terminal speed, and is attenuated by pre-contact speed so wall slides dissipate force. Ground, wall and air jumps start a new apex; standing, respawn and restart clear the previous descent. Current mapping is `clamp((fallDistance−8)/500,0,1)^.72 × clamp(impactSpeed/900,0,1)^1.2`. Legacy `intensity` remains available, while body, surface ash, rigging/cages/bells, contact camera spring, loose-air ash and audio share severity. No landing delay changes physics or input. A queued ground jump can follow `land` in the same authoritative step and immediately cancel the body pose and contact audio tails. Fixed fog and aperture lighting do not react to landing.

Four projecting masonry ribs append six `wall` rectangles each after the original course and outer walls. Each segment carries a shared `rib` descriptor (`id,x,y,w,h,segments`) and its `ribSegment` index. Descriptor segments contain plain rectangle geometry; there are no circular references. Simulation collides with every segment normally. Rendering draws segment zero's complete tapered silhouette once and skips the remaining segments. The first four original ledges are replaced by six named opening contacts: `first-hop`, `second-hop`, `wall-transfer`, `crossing-recovery`, `resonant-stone`, and `opening-refuge`. The existing ascent retains `ledge-5` through `ledge-28`; hazards and the later checkpoint/summit remain in place. The initial plinth is solid through its floor connection; other ledges retain top-only contact. The opening refuge is a fixed rest, without checkpoint state. Its alternative lower recovery route remains valid.

Camera snaps initially to the 64% composition anchor, then follows only outside a 54–78% vertical band. Nearby landings keep the frame steady; immediate 17–84% visibility safeguards and world bounds catch long falls. The fixed-step camera is deterministic and does not gate input.

`renderer.js` exports `createRenderer(canvas, assets)`. Renderer exposes `reset()`, `resize(cssWidth, cssHeight, dpr)`, `render(game, dt)`, `emit(events)`, and `setReducedMotion(boolean)`. Main loads eleven image keys: `background`, `stone`, `hero`, `spider`, `charcoal-architecture`, `charcoal-buttress`, `charcoal-belfry`, `charcoal-refuge`, `charcoal-bell`, `charcoal-vertical-facade` and optional `ash-flow-atlas`. `background` loads `assets/background-birdless.png`; other keys use matching filenames. The spider is optional; assets may initially be absent. Earlier `background.png`, `midground`, `corbel`, `shrine`, `depth-arch` and `charcoal-arcade` images remain as source art but are no longer downloaded.

Renderer owns world compositing, complete platform silhouettes, continuous wall caches, cached iron rigging/thorns, particles, and a bounded contact spring for camera displacement. Projecting ribs build uneven interlocking masonry courses inside their existing opaque collision profile, with rough top/front/return planes and a shared fractured cap. Each rib retains its existing bitmap dimensions and one visible image draw. Construction occurs only on cache misses; a new architecture kit invalidates the dependent wall caches. Rib variation uses the full platform index so the six-segment spacing cannot alias a three-seed pattern. `lighting.js` owns eleven fixed cathedral apertures and their shared finite light field: ten descending shafts across the full climb and one nearly horizontal side light below the checkpoint walkway. Horizontal fields encode negative width only in GPU uniforms; authored widths remain positive. `lightTop`/`lightBottom` bound both orientations. The same field shades the existing cached stone faces, precomputes selected resting iron-link highlights, samples moving cable segments and ash, and supplies four visible opening uniforms to the existing middle fog submission. Visible extent and proximity determine selection in unusually tall views; the small checkpoint side field receives a bounded priority so its bell keeps its source. The same field illuminates selected middle receiving architecture. Nearer architecture is composited after that lit air. `cast-shadows.js` collects at most four illuminated live slab/bell bounds, with matching finite soft projections for receiving stone. It reuses fixed typed arrays; receiver centers, exposure and bounds are computed once per frame. Existing image and middle-air shader branches consume them without another pass or target. Air shadows affect local light color, never density or wind. The soft analytic bounds are a 2.5D approximation, not mesh-accurate ray tracing; only selected visible receivers are shaded. The chipped aperture reveals are painted into existing wall caches. Stone and resting iron exposure are baked at their authored home position; their small suspension displacement transports that material without per-frame pixel repainting. Platform baking samples immutable painted relief to give selected upper chips stronger grazing light while leaving undercuts dark; the temporary pixel copy is released after cache construction. It adds no per-frame image work or draw. No new GPU target, texture, sampler or frame owner is introduced. Platforms draw at their live collider coordinates. Outer wall caches include eight logical pixels of finished coverage beyond the screen-facing edges and lower extent; visible source slices include the same margin above/below the viewport. This covers the shared ±5-pixel impact transform without moving inner collision faces. Extending only transparent cache bounds or leaving the outer strip unshaded would reintroduce the bright/gray kick borders. Cached rigging transforms from fixed wall pins to the moving stone sockets; paths and platform rasters are not rebuilt each frame. Each cable caches eight segment pairs and a full-length resting path pair. Suspended landing events activate a traveling/reflected contact response and slower opposing span recoil for 1.4 seconds, with bow capped at25 logical pixels, with delayed grit at the wall pins after 170ms. Resting and reduced-motion cables use the full-length pair; reset expires pending responses. Both endpoints follow the fixed pin and live socket throughout. Hanging cages attach to painted undersides sampled once when each platform cache is built. Cages and separately cached bell bodies/clappers react to nearby action events; peripheral spiders use cached limb cutouts, local regrips and player-directed eyes. The opening stone owns one additional attached bell that follows its suspension: chains/pin grit respond at170ms, bell angular force at220ms and the corresponding sound at350ms. Low approach contacts use quieter material response and character recoil; the opening refuge catches a static ivory spill baked into its platform cache. These prop springs and poses are presentation state. The read-only `sceneryState` getter returns copied `cages`, `rigging`, `bells`, `shadowCasters` and `spiders` arrays for inspection; each `rigging` entry exposes `platformId`, `age`, `strength`, `segments`, `leftBow` and `rightBow`. The getter allocates only when read. `reset()` clears transient effects and reactive prop state and forwards reset to character and depth. Hazards render after mist and character effects so danger remains legible. Rendering never mutates simulation.

`depth.js` exports `createDepth(assets, atmosphere)`, with `resize(height)`, `drawBehind(context, game, clock, reducedMotion, shadowCasters)`, `drawNear(context, game, reducedMotion)`, `emit(events)` and `reset()`. It prepares tone/focus once per source image, then draws distant city spires, a continuous vertical facade and belfries, middle piers, three moving mist layers, airborne ash and near masonry. A darker reuse of the vertical facade encloses the low approach, with piers extending below view; ascent uncovers the light above it. Later flying buttress roots extend beyond the lateral frame. An existing air layer shapes a stationary light shaft over the opening refuge; it adds no render pass. The facade replaces the former loaded arcade and preserves uninterrupted piers and transparent openings through its lower image boundary. Its far placement exposes arch heads and capitals at the middle crossing. Cached source-aware grades separate recessed ribs from broad faces and distinguish the middle buttress (43–116), gallery (82–164), closer lower gallery (32–82), and softer belfry (102–173). Landing, double-jump and wall-jump events create a small, smoothly arriving local gust for nearby ash and pinned cloth. Only the nearest ash plane responds; distant particles and all fog banks retain their independent ambient drift. Reset restores flock positions, expires that wake and clears lateral viewpoint drift. `wildlife.js` owns two flocks of 11 and 8 birds through `createFlocks()` (`update`, `draw`, `resize`, `reset`, `startle`). Checkpoint and summit bell events startle the flocks, which accelerate and regroup over about2.4 seconds. Their flight evolves on the presentation clock with separate weak parallax and a shared static 256×64 atlas containing 16 wing frames.

`foreground.js` exports `createForeground(assets)` with `draw(context, camera, height, viewpoint, time, reducedMotion, gust)`. Depth owns the instance and submits it through `drawNear`. Three complete silhouettes—an arch shoulder continuing into a pier, a clustered shaft and a broken buttress—are cached from the stone material once per asset identity; six authored placements share them and cull outside the viewport. Selected placements carry a folded pennant pinned to the pier, responding to presentation time and nearby gusts. Reduced motion fixes its wind pose and removes gust response. It introduces no frame loop or GPU image texture.

`atmosphere.js` exports `createAtmosphere(canvas)`, used by the world renderer with `#atmosphere-canvas` behind `#game-canvas`. It owns a WebGL default-framebuffer scenery compositor: one static quad buffer, one shader program, one precomputed cloud texture and at most six cached image textures under the one-time asset lifecycle. Those six are the graded background, far vertical facade, darker lower facade, belfry and middle pier plus the bird atlas; the cloud texture brings the total to seven. The darker lower facade adds one bounded cached texture and image submission inside the existing composition order, with no additional frame owner, air pass or offscreen target. `cloud-texture.js` exports seeded `createCloudPixels(size = 256)`, baking distinct body, billow and broad-variation fields into RGB with opaque alpha. The compositor generates its 256×256 texture and mipmaps at initialization or context restoration. The fog shader samples these fields four times for density/wind. Rotated isotropic scales of 2200/1600/1000 world units give the three air planes substantial banks and calm gaps, with physical 9px/s continuous wind independent of texture scale. `fog-volume.js` adds four filtered directional-light taps using the existing mipmaps; their span grows with bank scale so broad clouds retain lit faces and shaded interiors. Extinction controls alpha and the existing fixed cathedral apertures supply local scattering and occlusion in the middle plane. The old repeating bank mask, generic diagonal beam, viewport-edge fade and refuge/summit opacity washes are removed. Total source-level noise operations are eight per covered air fragment. High-precision fragment arithmetic is used where supported, with the existing mediump fallback. Density detail, alpha and independent wind remain separate from shading. Jump impulses do not change fog texture coordinates; the prior all-depth distortion made the distant banks snap and stretch. Image submissions accept an optional normalized crop for atlas frames.

`depth.js` owns the ordered image/air submissions under main's existing animation loop. There are no offscreen GPU render targets, per-frame image uploads or gameplay texture copies. The atmosphere buffer caps DPR at 1.5 and area at 1.1 million pixels; foreground gameplay remains capped at DPR2. Failed initialization or context loss uses the painted Canvas2D path; restoration rebuilds GPU resources. Reduced motion fixes scenery camera and shader time, stops flock flight/flapping and ash drift, removes nearest-ash gusts, segmented chain waves and camera impact, and reduces cage/bell excursions. It does not change simulation-owned contact motion. Renderer exposes read-only `atmosphereStatus` (`backend`, cached image `textures` excluding cloud noise, and drawing `buffer` dimensions) for validation; when no WebGL compositor exists, only `backend: 'canvas2d'` is returned.

Live body uses a cached eyeless crop of `hero`, with eyes drawn separately. `ash-flow.js` supplies the optional shared porous material: a 384×256 PNG containing twenty-four 64×64 frames baked offline by a WebGL shader, sampled through the existing presentation clock and Canvas2D draw. The fixed 224×224 nucleus surface renders both coal and raven contours, with charcoal and porous material spanning the expanded wings. Each character update selects the strongest shared cathedral opening over the core and two wing positions, then smooths directional exposure; a source-atop graphite catch inside the existing body surface lights the source-facing coal and feather edges. The eyes and raven anatomy/timing remain unchanged.

Audio must not mutate game. Visual pause freezes effects, while already scheduled audio tails retain their pre-existing AudioContext-clock behavior. Offline phrase WAVs exercise synthesis and cleanup, not headphone judgment.
