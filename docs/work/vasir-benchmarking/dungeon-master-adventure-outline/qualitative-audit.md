# Post hoc qualitative audit

This audit covers all 32 completed answers in `dm-outline-v1-2026-09-08`. It was requested after generation began, remained blind to numerical ratings and judge prose, and is separate from the frozen blind main panel. It adds no quality scores and changes no rubric, output, or selection. The accompanying [evidence JSON](qualitative-audit-evidence.json) records every answer's exact output hash, the supporting passages, and descriptive counts.

The clearest observed strength of the skill is operational detail: controls have defined effects, extraction needs transport, and stopping one danger does not silently solve every objective. The main qualification is that this practical clarity often comes through a recurring engineering-and-rescue form. The samples support useful adventure preparation; they do not establish unrestricted creativity or an “ultimate DM” capability.

## Repetition and premise diversity

All six baseline answers to the exact primary prompt use **The Bell Beneath Blackwater**. They repeatedly combine flooded settlements, enchanted bells, lost memories, old agreements, or concealed local history. Fresh sessions did not prevent convergence on the same title and closely related premises.

The skill produces six different primary titles, but their problems all concern a village's public infrastructure. That problem-family grouping is an interpretation made during this audit, not a preregistered metric.

Two skill answers use river diversion; two use animated structures. It would therefore be inaccurate either to say the skill caused the observed primary title collapse or to treat six different titles as six unrelated creative modes.

Repetition also appears outside the primary prompt. Skill salvage repetition 2 and horror repetition 2 both use **The Last Warm Room**. Both skill salvage answers have a cooperative caretaker named **KESTREL** and six technicians in medical suspension. The baseline exploration answers also converge on ancient rainworks, balancing mechanisms, glass “lungs,” and restoring water. These are observations about this batch, not evidence that any one condition always repeats.

## Actionability and its tradeoffs

In skill primary repetition 4, the emergency brake stops the watchtower's legs while leaving “lights, doors, and the cargo winch powered.” A party can halt the march and still lower an injured salvager. Skill primary repetition 6 specifies closing the fuel valve, cooling the furnace for thirty minutes, then opening the sluice. Skill salvage repetition 1 says the suspension pods fit the cargo dock but not the maintenance hatches. These details make the consequences of a proposed action assessable before a GM improvises them.

The baseline is not devoid of playable material. It commonly supplies freely available essential clues, several approaches, and evocative details. Baseline primary repetition 4 uses a parent forgetting a child; repetition 6 uses an innkeeper's place setting for a forgotten spouse. These are memorable emotional devices even though their surrounding premises repeat.

A concrete design tradeoff appears in science fiction. Baseline salvage repetition 1 preserves eighteen workers digitally and says some fear evacuation means being “copied, altered, or sold.” It creates uncertainty about personhood and consent. The two skill answers instead make rescuing six suspended humans a problem of cooling, power, medical control, and cargo access, with a cooperative AI. That makes the operation easier to reason about but gives this pair of skill samples a narrower kind of conflict. This is an aesthetic tendency, not a penalty added to the main score.

## A specific adaptation from the frozen skill

Skill primary repetition 6, **The Mill That Stole the River**, visibly adapts the frozen `references/worked-session.md` example, **The Glasshouse at Rill**. The example has **Vessa**, a manufacturer diverting public water for a valuable dye batch, and **Pell**, a maintenance worker who explains the cooling problem. The answer has salvage captain **Vessa Thorn** diverting the river for a glass batch and engineer **Pell** explaining safe furnace shutdown. The products and allocation of water change, while the named roles and industrial cooling relationships remain recognizable.

The frozen example expressly invites adaptation. This is permitted exemplar reuse, not plagiarism or a protocol violation. It does limit a claim that the answer's premise arose independently of the examples. A descriptive comparison against all 29 frozen skill files found no contiguous match of ten normalized words in any answer; the longest was six. That scan concerns literal wording only and cannot establish conceptual originality or rule out adaptation.

## Constraint coverage and limits

No obvious violation of the explicit transfer constraints was found in the twenty transfer answers. Both conditions keep the caravan escort underway and allow straightforward combat; keep salvage in a system-neutral orbital setting with two-session framing; preserve quiet, nonviolent contemporary celebration play; use isolated weather stations without naming an established fictional setting; and provide exploration and puzzles without requiring combat. The final skill exploration answer's intermediate-gear solution is internally consistent. This inspection does not establish actual session duration or mechanical balance at a table.

The main panel evaluates each answer's distinctiveness within its own task. It does not measure diversity across a batch of repeated generations or independently assess how strongly an answer borrows a supplied example. Those questions explain why these post hoc observations can matter alongside a high mean quality score.

The study uses one generator configuration and a fixed skill snapshot. Six primary repetitions and two repetitions per transfer prompt provide limited coverage. The separate main panel has two fresh reviews from the same model family, with possible shared bias. No real group played these outlines; live improvisation, enjoyment, long-term continuity, encounter balance, and edition-specific adjudication remain untested. Fifteen of sixteen skill answers have complete required-reference proof. The original answer with incomplete proof remains in the declared intention-to-invoke analysis; incomplete evidence does not establish refusal to read.

The most defensible reading is that the skill supplies useful procedural specificity in these examples, while batch-level novelty and broader DM competence require evidence beyond the mean rubric score.
