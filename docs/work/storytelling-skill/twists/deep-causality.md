# Deeper causal design for twists and revelations

This revision memo develops the causal and structural argument for `twists-and-revelations.md`. Its central proposal is to make the apparent story and concealed story produce one another. A hidden mastermind becomes dramatically substantial when other people's actions force him to make choices, those choices generate the misleading evidence, and the consequences eventually make continued concealment impossible or intolerably expensive. The revelation then exposes a struggle that was already happening and creates the conditions of the next struggle.

The current reference already establishes valuable distinctions: action versus interpretation; concealed agency versus unlimited control; restored understanding versus surviving injury; hierarchy versus evidence of hierarchy. Its remaining weakness is integration. Instructions such as “give the hidden character limits,” “change the next encounter,” and “let evidence have two lives” appear beside one another. A stronger chapter demonstrates how the limits produce the evidence, how the evidence shapes decisions, and how those decisions create the encounter in which the truth becomes available.

All Star Wars developments below are invented alternatives. They make no claims about canon or abandoned production plans. “Source doctrine” identifies McKee's claims; “inherited” identifies arguments already developed in the workspace; “new application” identifies the further deductions proposed here.

## 1. The expectation gap must continue through the secret

**Source doctrine.** A character chooses an action from a subjective estimate of what will work. The actual response contradicts that estimate, requiring a new action undertaken with greater risk. The person's revised understanding supplies the expectation that the next encounter tests. This is a progressive causal sequence, not a collection of surprising outcomes. McKee also directs the writer to inhabit another participant's perspective to discover the consequential reaction. [*Story*, physical pp.142–152](../../../../../writing/books/STORY__mckee-md/final/source/original.pdf#page=142); [pp.176–179](../../../../../writing/books/STORY__mckee-md/final/source/original.pdf#page=176).

**Inherited.** The canonical analysis interprets the gap as a collision between accounts of the situation and develops unexpected cooperation as a possible complication. The synthesis already explains how a success creates dependencies and how changing conditions can close routes of evasion. Those are not discoveries of this memo.

**New application.** Run this causal sequence through both sides of the concealment. The protagonist acts on an incomplete account of the antagonist. The antagonist acts on an incomplete account of the protagonist. The antagonist may know the secret, but he does not know the future. If the hero unexpectedly refuses a helpful escort, the hidden agent must choose another way to get the witness alone. That adaptation can cost access, damage an alliance, or require a more conspicuous intervention. Its visible residue becomes a clue because it was the least costly remaining way to pursue the objective.

The first interpretation should cause choices, not merely accompany them. If Nera regards Jar Jar as a harmless dependent, she may discuss the witness transfer while helping him gather scattered papers. He learns the route through her specific decision. If she later treats the interruption as evidence of his inability to work alone, she assigns him to accompany a trusted aide. The conclusion designed to manage the nuisance supplies his next access. A mistaken account has become part of the action's machinery.

The counterfactual is revealing: if Nera had known the truth earlier, which consequential choice would have differed? If none would, the concealment may still support an enjoyable audience puzzle, but it has not yet caused the lived predicament the chapter claims for it.

## 2. Two histories must share causes, not merely dates

The current phrase “concealed history” can encourage writing a second chronology beneath the first: while the heroes travel, the villain secretly arranges everything. Chronological overlap is insufficient. The concealed drama needs its own pressures, and the visible drama must deliver some of them.

Give Jar Jar two connected desires: obtain the strategic result and preserve the mistaken understanding that permits him to obtain it cheaply. These aims cooperate at first. His seeming helplessness gets him carried into guarded spaces; his intervention protects the plan; its clumsy appearance renews the explanation of helplessness. Later, the aims separate. A hero's victory removes a covert option. A subordinate's ambition outruns the schedule. An ally notices that the accidents repeatedly preserve the same asset. Jar Jar must surrender an advantage to keep his mask or endanger his mask to preserve the advantage.

That choice creates more than a plausible secret. It gives the concealed character something to lose in the scenes we already watch. The slip can be an actual failure whose consequences he salvages. The excessive joke can be an attempt to regain attention after somebody refuses the expected cue. On rereading, comedy becomes anxious labor as well as manipulation: we can see how close his intervention came to failing.

The hero's victory should remain a victory. If Nera removes Palpatine's access to government reinforcements, his loss of those forces remains real when the hidden master supplies a substitute. The new supply has a price and changes the relationship. Calling the original defeat “all part of the plan” throws away the opportunity for development. The defeat is what forces the plan to become something else.

This extends McKee's elimination of previously adequate methods: once a class of action has failed, progression requires a different quality or magnitude of action. The useful inheritance is the exhaustion of options; it need not make every subsequent event louder. [*Story*, physical pp.207–209](../../../../../writing/books/STORY__mckee-md/final/source/original.pdf#page=207).

## 3. Rebuild the Jar Jar revelation around earned exposure

The existing fleet scene establishes command through costly obedience. Preserve that. Its strongest image is the familiar sleeve tug stopping the hand of the apparent supreme strategist. But the current scene arrives with much of its governing predicament explained immediately beforehand, and Nera's access depends chiefly on her being in the passage. A fully integrated version could establish the following causal movement across earlier scenes.

First, Nera believes Palpatine's authority rests on his control of public institutions. She obtains testimony that can persuade the Senate's commanders to refuse an unlawful deployment. Jar Jar tries to interrupt the transfer of that testimony through an apparently comic mishap. Nera refuses to abandon the witness. His attempted delay fails; the testimony arrives. Her judgment about the immediate priority is correct, and that correctness materially harms the conspiracy.

Second, the refusal deprives Palpatine of forces he expected to command. His coup is already moving, so he now needs the privately financed fleet whose existence and logistical independence earlier scenes have established. Jar Jar controls its release. He did not desire the loss of public reinforcements, but he can exploit the dependence that loss creates. Palpatine's growing need gives him the leverage to demand that Skywalker be sacrificed before the replacement force is committed.

Third, Nera follows the failure of her own otherwise successful measure. The lawful commanders held; the coup continues. That discrepancy directs her investigation toward the independent supply. Earlier work with the command office gives her a credible way into the service passage or access to the relevant channel. She reaches the revelation by pursuing the consequences of her action.

Fourth, Palpatine attempts to secure the fleet while preserving Skywalker. The old terms would let him do both. Jar Jar changes the terms because an independent apprentice would diminish the dependence through which he rules Palpatine. He must now enforce command openly enough to overcome resistance. The same gesture that previously seemed an interruption becomes an order because we see the desired action stop and the costly alternative follow.

Finally, Nera discovers that her successful attack on formal power intensified a concealed dependence. Her victory has neither disappeared nor solved the problem. It has brought the deeper structure into view. She must now decide how to act without treating another impressive title as a complete map of power.

This sequence is a proposal, not an instruction to add five explanatory scenes. Existing scenes could carry several links. Its new contribution is the shared chain: a failed diversion permits a real political victory; that victory changes a dependency; the dependency produces a coercive demand; enforcing the demand exposes authority; the observer's pursuit makes her present to discover it. The hidden plot becomes discoverable because it had to answer the apparent plot.

## 4. Information changes some values immediately and others only through action

**Source doctrine.** McKee permits scenes to turn on revelation and shows past events furnishing present turning points. His scene account nonetheless defines the turn through a changed condition of life. [*Story*, physical pp.232–234](../../../../../writing/books/STORY__mckee-md/final/source/original.pdf#page=232); [pp.338–339](../../../../../writing/books/STORY__mckee-md/final/source/original.pdf#page=338).

**Inherited.** The synthesis already separates an event, its discovery, and its meaning. Learning that a door is locked can change a plan; locking it created the physical constraint. Learning of deception can itself change trust.

**New application.** Decompose the fleet scene's simultaneous movements. Jar Jar's superior authority existed before Nera heard of it. Palpatine's acceptance of the demand is a new choice. The bombardment order newly threatens the dock through an actionable command; it does not establish that Skywalker has died. Nera's discovery changes what she knows and may destroy confidence in her map of power. Her warning, if undertaken, could change what the threatened people can do. These are connected events with different causes and different times.

The information event becomes a value event when the knowledge changes a consequential relation, capacity, commitment, or possibility. A daughter learns that her father kept her letters; she may recover a sense of having been loved without receiving another letter or recovering the dead man. A witness learns who commands the fleet; she cannot revoke its orders merely by understanding them. Precision prevents explanation from receiving credit for a rescue it has not performed.

It also prevents the reverse mistake: forcing an additional chase onto an ending whose governing pursuit concerns recognition. Knowledge can be the event the work has made us need. The question is what kind of result the story promised, and whether this disclosure actually provides it.

## 5. Qualitative antagonism changes the available remedy

**Source doctrine.** Negation of the negation describes a compounded negative, a condition qualitatively worse. McKee distinguishes lawbreaking from tyranny's control over law and describes hatred masquerading as love and slavery perceived as freedom. [*Story*, physical pp.315–327](../../../../../writing/books/STORY__mckee-md/final/source/original.pdf#page=315).

**Inherited.** The analysis and synthesis draw a common mechanism from several of those examples: harm can capture the means by which people recognize, contest, or repair it. This is their interpretation, not McKee's universal definition of every item in his tables.

**New application.** A Sith above Palpatine is not automatically a deeper negative condition. Another villain with more ships may demand greater force while leaving the old remedy intact. A qualitatively deeper revelation shows why the remedy itself can serve the domination.

Suppose the heroes routinely ask the apparently harmless companion to help identify which officials deserve trust. His recommendations have not merely obstructed investigation; they have organized the investigators' confidence. Exposing a corrupt minister through his help may remove a rival and strengthen his preferred successor. The danger lies in entrusting the method of correction to someone whose conduct has not been examined. Greater sincerity, effort, and courage within that arrangement can increase his influence.

Such a revelation should therefore alter the heroes' practice of resistance. They may need independent witnesses, separated channels, competing interpretations, or ways to verify an instruction without asking its beneficiary to certify it. These are possible dramatic acts, not an institutional reform checklist. A single refusal to let the familiar helper carry the decisive message can embody the change if the whole story has made that transfer meaningful.

Do not locate the evil in compassion toward a ridiculous person. Compassion need not be the failed principle. Unexamined delegation of judgment can be the vulnerable practice. Nor should the eventual inference become “harmless-looking people are dangerous.” The more adequate judgment distinguishes kindness, dependency, competence, authority, and purpose rather than treating one social impression as evidence for all five.

## 6. New direction does not yet constitute crisis

The existing Nera ending makes the disclosure active: she must reach Dock Nine. That is a changed task. McKee's Crisis requires the final consequential dilemma, with incompatible goods or competing evils made concrete by the accumulated conditions. The source also permits another gap to open within the climactic action, demanding renewed improvisation. [*Story*, physical pp.302–307](../../../../../writing/books/STORY__mckee-md/final/source/original.pdf#page=302).

A stronger continuation could make Nera choose between preserving the last opportunity to establish the hidden ruler's authority and leaving immediately to interrupt the killing. But this is a dilemma only if both cannot reasonably be accomplished. Time, access, communication, and credibility must establish the incompatibility. An inexplicably absent recording function or a sudden refusal to send a message would manufacture the crisis through omission.

The knowledge changes the price of each option. Before the reveal, stopping Palpatine seemed to finish the undertaking. Afterward, preserving proof against him alone may leave the governing power untouched. Conversely, chasing proof while allowing the dock's destruction would repeat the master's habit of spending people to secure a future position. The revelation can thus expose a conflict between the protagonist's method and the very value the method was intended to serve.

Let the decision take time proportionate to its weight. McKee expressly describes the crisis as a held moment: action pauses while the audience experiences alternatives. A revelation scene can slow physically and intensify dramatically. The defect is not pause itself; it is information that neither deepens the pending decision nor develops an independently compelling experience. [*Story*, physical p.307](../../../../../writing/books/STORY__mckee-md/final/source/original.pdf#page=307).

## 7. Honest irony preserves an actual gain and an actual loss

McKee requires an ironic climax's positive and negative declarations to be distinct, motivated, and connected through the action. He separately treats ironic progression as the precise inversion of a person's expected route to desire. [*Story*, physical pp.126–130](../../../../../writing/books/STORY__mckee-md/final/source/original.pdf#page=126); [pp.297–300](../../../../../writing/books/STORY__mckee-md/final/source/original.pdf#page=297).

The fleet scene offers a defensible local irony: Palpatine gains the means to seize public power by accepting private submission. The ships really become available, and Skywalker really represented a possible route toward reduced dependence. Both consequences arise from the same acceptance. That is stronger than supplying a victory and then appending unrelated bad news.

But the wording must match the established timeline. If Palpatine always belonged to Jar Jar, he does not newly become an apprentice when Nera discovers it. His present submission can deepen or ratify the bondage by destroying an available escape. To say he “gains power and loses freedom” requires establishing the particular freedom he could have exercised before this choice.

Likewise, an audience's gain in knowledge does not automatically balance a character's death into a dual outcome. The benefit belongs to a different subject. That arrangement may be tragic and artistically powerful, but its accounts must remain explicit. A surviving character's recovered esteem for a dead protector can be a real present gain, accompanied by loss of the possibility of mutual recognition. Neither restores the dead person.

The current reference rightly preserves genuine kindness within manipulation. Develop the causal implication: the real care may be what makes the harm effective. Ruth's accurate knowledge of Mara's voice permits both sabotage and a tender rescue. The tenderness is not evidence that sabotage was necessary, and the sabotage does not prove every remembered pleasure counterfeit. The same competence can make someone indispensable and make their attempted separation dangerous. An ironic account gains depth by specifying this relationship instead of awarding positive and negative adjectives to the same person.

## 8. A powerful revelation may be the penultimate climax

McKee explicitly imagines a brother's betrayal as the penultimate climax, followed by an act in which the betrayed brother answers it. Elsewhere he distinguishes scene, sequence, act, and whole-story reversals and insists that the ending fulfill the emotional anticipation the telling created. [*Story*, physical pp.233–234](../../../../../writing/books/STORY__mckee-md/final/source/original.pdf#page=233); [p.241](../../../../../writing/books/STORY__mckee-md/final/source/original.pdf#page=241); [pp.308–313](../../../../../writing/books/STORY__mckee-md/final/source/original.pdf#page=308).

The hierarchy scene climaxes a contest between Jar Jar and Palpatine: the subordinate submits and the fleet moves. For Nera it can climax discovery while launching a more difficult action. These are different structural jobs. Calling the scene “the climax” without identifying whose pursuit it resolves can obscure the unfinished story.

If the whole work promises the rescue of the Republic, “Jar Jar was above him” generally leaves that promise unsettled. The final movement must show what the discovery enables or fails to enable. If the work instead promises an explanation of how the Republic was captured, the revelation could supply its governing conclusion, provided the prior action has already established the political outcome and the new knowledge completes its causal meaning.

A final line therefore carries a specific burden. It may compress the recognition of a completed tragedy; it may leave the consequence immediately imaginable; or it may announce the next story. Those experiences differ. The reference should teach the writer to decide which ending is being made before polishing the line that names the secret.
