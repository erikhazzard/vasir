## Overall finding

The reference can guide actual writing and revision. Its strongest contributions are specific decisions a writer can execute: separate observation from inference; identify the assumption the reveal overturns; give a remembered detail a present purpose and a later explanation; construct a feasible concealed history; distinguish what revealing evidence establishes from what someone merely claims; and write the consequence of recognition. Keep those operations. The remaining need is a continuous worked demonstration, accompanied by less repeated qualification. A demonstration would make the many correct distinctions easier to use together.

The master route table currently does not name the new specialist. When integrating it, give retrospective revelations a direct route and distinguish that route from the existing suspense/mystery route. The latter already promises fair clues and suspense, so “twists” should route by the uncertainty it uniquely resolves: an earlier act receiving a consequential second interpretation.

## Substantive reference improvements

1. **Carry one example through construction and revision.** The reference currently supplies several valuable miniature examples, then changes examples before they have to satisfy all its requirements together. Its eight-step development sequence is actionable, but no completed passage yet demonstrates their interaction. Use the Jar Jar expansion for the extended causal construction and a short original passage for the experience on the page. Neither needs to become a checklist. In the original passage, explicitly identify the initial inference, one remembered detail, the disclosure's actual evidentiary reach, and the protagonist's changed action.

2. **Concentrate repeated qualifications where they change a decision.** Preserving genuine injury despite good allegiance appears in the opening, the recognition/value section, the concealed-character section, dialogue, irony, Snape, and the repair table. Limited mastermind agency and the distinction between skill and command recur similarly. Some recurrence helps a reference read out of order, but the current repeated restatements slow its argument. Keep a full explanation in the owning section and retain elsewhere only the application that adds something: humiliation in dialogue, irreversible death in Snape, independent political choices in Jar Jar. The repair table earns its repetition because it supports targeted revision.

3. **Keep interpretive provenance clear without interrupting every example.** The Snape section already announces that sourced plot facts and craft interpretation differ; subsequent repeated “our reading” and “our craft inference” can be reduced where the paragraph unmistakably performs analysis. Likewise, the opening and first Jar Jar paragraph sufficiently establish the alternative-story boundary. Keep that boundary next to any newly introduced hypothetical scene, but the final source discussion need not restate it at length. The note about an official feature's unrelated factual error belongs in the research record unless it affects a fact used in the runtime example. Preserve the primary-source locators and the warning against treating book prescriptions as universal rules.

4. **Preserve the disclosure section's proportional standard in the worked example.** This is a strength that the proposal currently undercuts in its explanation. The reference correctly permits emotional recognition without a forensic prosecution and rejects the leap from evidence of a capacity to proof of ultimate responsibility. The example should accordingly distinguish the recording's observation, the experiment's causal result, and the confrontation's disclosure of motive. Do not describe one of these as logically establishing all three.

The main conceptual relationships are coherent. Event, recognition, and later response can change different values. Restricted access differs from false interior narration; an honestly occupied secret keeper need not think a secret aloud in every scene. The text also allows a final revelation to change judgment or mourning without manufacturing another action climax. These distinctions should survive compression.

## “Our Song”: precise repairs

### Make the setup less diagnostic

The first paragraph announces the exact sabotage mechanism: capo higher than the second fret, verses possible, last chorus line impossible. The performance then spotlights Ruth moving the capo, her hands being hidden, and the brighter sound. These clues converge so conspicuously that the scene risks announcing sabotage before the emotional first interpretation has had time to become credible. This does not make the story invalid—foreknown betrayal can hurt—but it weakens its usefulness as the reference's demonstration of two initially plausible readings.

Replace the rule-like opening with a useful shared success, for example:

> At soundcheck Ruth had found her key: capo behind the second fret, and the last line stopped being work. “There,” Ruth had said. “That's yours.” Mara was still holding on to the ease of that note.

This prepares the correct position and Ruth's knowledge of Mara's range through care that matters now. It does not immediately tell the reader exactly how to break the coming performance. Let the later experiment make the transposition consequence explicit. This is a suggested replacement, not a claim that the revised scene has already been written.

### Let attention explain what Mara misses

“The microphone blocked Mara's view of her hands” is an unnecessarily engineered occlusion. An ordinary microphone and stand would not readily hide the relevant hand movement and guitar-neck position; the stage arrangement is unspecified. It also draws attention to the withheld observation.

Keep the visible preparation and redirect Mara's attention toward the very opportunity that matters to her:

> Ruth adjusted the capo. Jo raised her phone, and Mara tried to smile at her. Then Ruth gave her the opening chord.

Mara can observe an adjustment without registering its precise fret. The already established phone can later preserve what her anxious attention missed. This uses the same viewpoint consistently and keeps the filming ordinary.

### Support dependence without making experience disappear

Six years performing together and a possible summer tour make Mara sound experienced. A three-semitone increase remains a reasonable objection for some versions of that experience. It is not necessary to claim that every practiced singer would identify the key immediately: relative pitch, range, song familiarity, and confidence are different. The scene already lets her register a brighter sound and rising difficulty while attributing trouble to nerves.

If this history is retained, clarify the specific dependence through an established habit rather than declaring her musically unskilled. For example, every previous performance may have begun on Ruth's cue, with Mara rarely choosing the starting pitch herself. If the larger work establishes Mara as a confident independent accompanist, the capo mechanism needs to change. The proposal's existing note recognizes this limit; the finished prose should carry the necessary characterization itself.

### Do not overstate the recording

Ruth's “I moved it for the next song” remains compatible with her checking the clamp: she could have prepared the wrong song deliberately but without intending sabotage. Mara's “You looked” makes a casual slip less persuasive; it does not eliminate an ordinary sequencing mistake. Reproducing the failed line proves the effect of the higher key, not Ruth's purpose.

The subsequent exchange can still disclose purpose perfectly well. “They weren't asking for both of us” abandons a purely technical defense and exposes the grievance. Preserve the movement into that admission. In the explanatory note, change “Ruth discloses only after the evidence defeats her attempted explanation” to something like: “Mara identifies Ruth's intervention, and Ruth answers the accusation with the grievance she has been concealing.” If stronger confirmation is desired, let Ruth voluntarily say why she chose the higher key; do not add an elaborate evidence device to an intimate scene.

### Correct the guitar's continuity

Mara has already taken the guitar from its case and used it to compare the two keys. Ruth's final “Let me get the guitar” treats the instrument as if it is still elsewhere. Change the offer to “I'll play for you.” Mara's “I can start it” then refuses the familiar dependency without asserting that she can now manage every part of touring. Keep the physical business consistent if Mara sets the guitar down to use her phone.

## Strengths of the miniature to preserve

The rescue really helps Mara after the sabotage; the affection and musicianship are not retrospectively erased. The recording has an ordinary first purpose, and Mara reviews it for an immediate consequential reason rather than stumbling into the answer. Ruth neither controls the audience nor guarantees the lost opportunity. Most importantly, Mara's response addresses the false conclusion the scheme produced: she requests another hearing and takes control of the starting pitch. She does not infer guaranteed professional success from the discovery of one engineered failure. The undecided doorway leaves the damaged relationship alive without pretending that deciding about the audition settles it.

## Final bounded reading of the integrated examples

### Jar Jar: the new scene performs the proposed reinterpretation

The expansion now shows the familiar comic behavior continuing during discovery. The dropped cups recruit assistance; the protagonist adopts the helping role; a concealed door, a waiting enemy, and a timing adjustment disclose what that role has been accomplishing. Jar Jar does not need to stop clowning, announce a new identity, or acquire a new ability. The protagonist's memory of having helped at the other thresholds gives the recognition a personal consequence: her ordinary cooperation has served a purpose she did not perceive. Staying where she can see the door changes that cooperation immediately.

The scene also respects its evidentiary scope. It shows deliberate access and coordination, while the surrounding explanation separately identifies what would establish larger strategy or authority over Palpatine. The protagonist has already seen the courier, and the physical passage needs no new technology. The abbreviated staging is workable; it does not require a diagram or an elaborate logistical explanation. The two earlier comic encounters remain preparation instructions, not prose demonstrated by this excerpt, and the reference accurately says they still need to work as scenes.

One exact viewpoint repair remains in this reading: “The guard straightens sooner than expected” quietly imports an unspecified expectation, most naturally Jar Jar's, into a scene otherwise restricted to observed conduct. Replace it with “The guard straightens and begins to turn toward the side door.” The next dropped cup then visibly interrupts a threatened sightline. This supports the explanatory claim of live adjustment without borrowing interior knowledge from the hidden agent.

### “Our Song”: the intended costly reading survives the repairs

The soundcheck now establishes the correct key through useful care rather than announcing an exact failure recipe. Mara's history of beginning on Ruth's cue supports the specific dependence at stake. “Then why did you start mine?” presses the discrepancy more credibly than “You looked,” and the explanation now assigns different jobs to the recording, experiment, and subsequent answer. “I'll play for you” correctly returns to the dependence between them without misplacing the guitar.

The first encounter still offers real relief, affection, competent accompaniment, and humiliation. The second encounter changes the cause assigned to that humiliation while preserving the value of the rescue and the harm of needing it. Mara's renewed request for a hearing and her uncertain response to Ruth in the doorway remain appropriately different decisions. No further conceptual repair is needed to make this a useful worked example.

Two small action-continuity issues remain in the inspected prose:

- Jo sits in the front row, but Mara later watches her raise the phone to clear “the head in front of her.” Replace the latter action with “Mara watched Jo turn the phone sideways and steady it in both hands.” That preserves ordinary divided attention without inventing a contradictory seating arrangement.
- Mara stops the video during the confrontation; the duet subsequently sounds from the phone without a resumption or a jump forward. Replace the later opening with “Mara skipped ahead in the recording. From the phone came the start of their duet.” This restores the device's action sequence and keeps the duet's emotional interruption.
