## P2 — The historian example does not yet earn its publication-versus-privacy dilemma

**Location:** `references/antagonism-and-irony.md:116–122`.

The setup gives Tomas's letters as the surviving evidence and his permission to examine them subject to privacy. The next paragraph says, “The museum correctly argues that unverifiable private assertions cannot change attribution,” then asserts that redaction fails because “the identifying dates are essential to proving authorship.” Sela subsequently publishes without permission.

Two missing links do substantial work here. First, essential dates do not, by themselves, explain why the evidence reveals an intimate relationship. Second, the museum's need for verification does not establish a need for *public* disclosure. Confidential examination of originals, authentication by a qualified third party, or a more selective excerpt remain apparent alternatives. The example treats their absence as a settled condition and labels the museum's demand correct before establishing the particular evidentiary problem. That risks manufacturing the dilemma the reference then uses to demonstrate earned irony.

This conflicts with the skill's own useful test in `references/plot-and-structure.md:128`: “What apparently easy third option has the story established, eliminated, or simply forgotten?” The problem is local: the proposed ironic outcome can work, but the presented setup does not yet require Sela's chosen breach.

**Action:** Add a brief, concrete reason why this particular evidence cannot establish authorship without identifying the protected relationship, and show why the most plausible confidential verification route fails under the museum's established circumstances. Avoid claiming a universal rule about museums. Alternatively, keep a viable privacy-preserving route and make Sela knowingly reject it for speed, control, or recognition; then revise the description of the dilemma to acknowledge her available alternative. That produces a different account of her responsibility and should be named as such.

**Completion check:** A reader can explain both why the protected fact is evidentially necessary and why publication is necessary *in this case*, or can identify the workable alternative Sela culpably refuses.

## P2 — The musician outline promotes an ambiguous exit into a confirmed cost

**Location:** `references/plot-and-structure.md:171, 174–175, 178`.

The director warns that a billing change will “jeopardize” an introduction. At crisis, the outline correctly says the alternative offers “no guarantee” of the producer's approval. The climax then presents “The producer leaves before the curtain call” and concludes that “an opportunity is genuinely lost.” The later explanation calls the cost clear.

A departure is observable; withdrawal of the opportunity is an inference. Nothing in the presented departure establishes that the producer is rejecting Ada, that the credit correction caused the departure, or that an introduction is no longer available. The sketch can intentionally leave those matters uncertain, but its explanation currently certifies the strongest reading. This is precisely the observation/interpretation distinction the master teaches elsewhere.

**Action:** Either establish withdrawal in an economical, observable way, with a prepared reason that makes the producer more than an automatic penalty for ethical behavior, or describe the outcome more modestly: Ada gives up assured sole billing and accepts career uncertainty. If a definite career loss is essential to the example, enact that loss rather than asking the exit to prove it.

**Completion check:** The asserted sacrifice matches what the outline establishes. The ending may contain uncertainty, but the commentary no longer calls that uncertainty a demonstrated lost opportunity.

## P3 — Broaden one substantial demonstration beyond negotiating legitimate claims

**Location:** the extended examples in `references/controlling-idea.md:145–161`, `plot-and-structure.md:165–180`, `scenes-and-beats.md:101–166`, `antagonism-and-irony.md:116–126`, `characters.md:126–160`, `character-arcs.md:60–102`, `relationships-and-cast.md:127–147`, `dialogue.md:93–151`, and `world-and-myth.md:44–72`. Compare the short mystery and horror treatments at `references/genre-and-form.md:141–169`.

The long demonstrations strongly cluster around a recognizable movement: a capable person controls access, knowledge, credit, or another person's future; a second person asserts an independent claim; a practical arrangement exposes or repairs the limit of control. The archive example's recurrence is useful for integration, and it is not itself a defect. The ferry, musician, keys, letters, and bell-office examples nevertheless repeat closely related ethical and conversational pressures. Their speakers repeatedly reach clear, compact formulations of permission, acknowledgment, and refusal.

The skill *does* allow other forms in operative instructions, and it includes actual quiet observational prose, comedy, tenderness, and voice variation. It would be inaccurate to call it uniformly moralistic or devoid of prose craft. The narrower risk is that an agent learning from its most developed demonstrations could repeatedly turn diverse premises into negotiations over authority and boundaries. Mystery and horror, for example, receive useful principles but no comparably developed positive demonstration of clue inference or escalating dread. No generation test was performed here, so that consequence remains a plausible risk rather than an observed result.

**Action:** Replace or supplement one repeated authority-centered demonstration with a bounded worked case whose engine is different: a fair material inference that changes an investigation; an antagonist adapting intelligently without becoming a thematic lecturer; or a sequence that produces dread through perception and delayed recognition. Show the actual passage and the consequential transitions. This does not require a full additional specialist or one example for every genre. Preserve the integrated archive example.

**Status:** Nonblocking breadth improvement. Preferring more violence, grander scale, darker outcomes, or more ornate prose would be taste, and none is required by this finding.
