# Performance Genre Reference

## Mandatory Genre Contract

> **If Performance is the global genre, the controlling idea’s Value must resolve `Shame / Respect`. Philosophy, character arc, setting, and subgenre may determine the Cause or secondary charges; they may not replace this axis.**

| Field | Contract |
|---|---|
| Global value axis | **Shame / Respect** |
| Positive pole | **Respect** |
| Negative pole | **Shame** |
| Core event | The Big Performance Scene: under decisive pressure, the protagonist unconditionally expresses—or betrays, withholds, or corrupts—their unique gift. |
| Core-event placement | Preferably at the global climax. |
| Core emotion | Triumph |
| Core need | Esteem and respect through genuine expression and mastery of the protagonist’s gift. |
| Conscious object of desire | Win or gain access to the major performance opportunity, Master and express the gift, Earn respect from audience, peers, mentor, or self, Defeat a rival or standard without surrendering the authentic gift |

## Value Spectrum — Operational Synthesis

The supplied source names the global poles. The deeper states below operationalize McKee’s contrary, contradictory, and negation-of-negation progression for this genre; tailor the exact wording to the story without leaving the canonical axis.

| Level | Genre state |
|---|---|
| Positive | Respect grounded in genuine mastery and expression |
| Contrary | Doubt, neglect, mediocrity, or lack of recognition |
| Contradictory | Shame, humiliation, or repudiation of the gift |
| Negation of the negation | Counterfeit respect purchased by self-erasure; public success that confirms private shame or destroys the gift |

## Core Roles — Source-Derived

- **Protagonist:** Pursues fulfillment, mastery, or recognition through expression of a unique gift.
- **Mentor:** Guides and challenges; may support, impose a false standard, or betray.
- **Antagonist / Rival:** Competes, judges, blocks opportunity, or embodies a destructive standard.
- **Society / Audience:** Creates external expectations and confers, withholds, or misunderstands respect.

## Core-Emotion Techniques — Source-Derived

1. Overcoming internal fear
2. Breaking societal expectations
3. Demonstrating mastery
4. Mentorship support or betrayal
5. Choosing fulfillment over external validation

## Genre Conventions — Source-Derived

- A strong mentor who guides and may challenge or betray.
- Rigorous training and repeated tests of the craft.
- An explicit All Is Lost moment linked to failure and ignominy.
- A large power divide between protagonist and rival, mentor, gatekeeper, or society.
- A win-but-lose or lose-but-win ending that separates external result from genuine respect.
- The Big Performance Scene as the culminating expression of the gift.

## Obligatory Moments — Source-Derived, Structurally Normalized

1. An inciting performance opportunity challenges the protagonist.
2. The protagonist sidesteps responsibility or resists the gift.
3. Forced into the spotlight, the protagonist resists or performs inadequately.
4. The protagonist learns the antagonist’s, mentor’s, or society’s standards and expectations.
5. The initial strategy to excel, imitate, please, or outshine fails.
6. At All Is Lost, the protagonist recognizes a needed change in strategy or attitude—or refuses it.
7. The Big Performance Scene unconditionally expresses or betrays the gift.
8. Respect is earned or Shame prevails, often with sacrifice at another value level.

## Conscious Objects of Desire — Source-Derived / Consolidated

- Win or gain access to the major performance opportunity
- Master and express the gift
- Earn respect from audience, peers, mentor, or self
- Defeat a rival or standard without surrendering the authentic gift

## Genre Spine

An opportunity exposes the protagonist’s gift and fear of judgment. Training, mentorship, rivalry, and social standards pressure the protagonist to imitate, compromise, or master. The initial strategy fails. The Big Performance then reveals whether the gift can be expressed without condition and whether the resulting acclaim, defeat, or sacrifice produces genuine Respect or Shame.

## Twenty-Skeletal-Scene Map — Operational Normalization

The supplied notes often use “climax” for a decision, condition, or encounter. This map enforces:

- **Crisis = choice.**
- **Climax = enacted answer.**
- **Resolution = consequence.**

| Command | Genre-specific function |
|---|---|
| Inciting Incident | An unexpected opportunity could reveal the protagonist’s gift. |
| Turning Point | An early setback or judgment exposes the gap between desire and present capacity. |
| Crisis | Accept the opportunity and risk shame, or withdraw and preserve the untested self-image. |
| Climax | The protagonist commits to the opportunity. |
| Resolution | The magnitude of training, gatekeeping, and judgment becomes clear. |

|---|---|

| Command | Genre-specific function |
|---|---|

| Climax | The protagonist commits to the final form of expression. |
| Resolution | Preparation for the Big Performance continues under maximal stakes. |
### Ending Payoff

| Command | Genre-specific function |
|---|---|
| Inciting Incident | The Big Performance begins with everything at stake. |
| Turning Point | An unexpected failure, rule, injury, betrayal, or demand makes the prepared performance impossible. |
| Crisis | Deliver the externally approved version for likely success, or express the authentic gift and risk public failure. |
| Climax | The protagonist performs, refuses, improvises, or compromises in the decisive public action. |
| Resolution | Respect or Shame becomes final, with a possible win/lose split between result, audience, relationship, and self-respect. |

## Adjacent-Genre Disambiguation

- Winning/losing, fame/obscurity, and success/failure are outcomes or secondary values; Performance’s global value is Shame/Respect.
- Choose Action when physical survival and defeat of a villain control the final event rather than expression of the gift before an evaluating audience or standard.
- Choose Love when the Proof of Love—not the Big Performance—controls the final value turn.

## Controlling-Idea Value Rules

- The controlling idea must resolve Respect or Shame.
- Triumph is the core emotion, not the controlling value.
- Mastery, authenticity, discipline, mentorship, teamwork, victory, and public acclaim belong in the Cause or secondary charge.
- External applause can coexist with a negative Shame ending; public defeat can coexist with positive Respect.
