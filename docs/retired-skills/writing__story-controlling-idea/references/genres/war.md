## Value Spectrum — Operational Synthesis

The supplied source names the global poles. The deeper states below operationalize McKee’s contrary, contradictory, and negation-of-negation progression for this genre; tailor the exact wording to the story without leaving the canonical axis.

| Level | Genre state |
|---|---|
| Positive | Honor: courageous, proportionate, humane action ordered toward worthy kinship or duty |
| Contrary | Fear, compromise, avoidance, divided loyalty, or failure of nerve |
| Contradictory | Dishonor: betrayal, cowardice, atrocity, abandonment, or corruption of duty |
| Negation of the negation | Atrocity celebrated as honor, or soldiers’ sacrifice manipulated into a lie that destroys the humanity it claims to defend |

## Core Roles — Source-Derived

- **Central Avatar:** Carries the principal honor conflict.
- **Offshoot Avatars / Unit:** Embodies competing facets of duty, fear, loyalty, ambition, and sacrifice.
- **Command / Institution:** Defines mission, rank, rules, and possible corruption of sacrifice.
- **Enemy / Antagonistic Force:** Pursues a military objective whose nature must be understood.
- **Kin / Comrades / Civilians:** Give sacrifice its human object and moral weight.

## Genre Conventions — Source-Derived

- One central avatar with offshoot avatars embodying multiple responses to war.
- A big external or internal canvas.
- Overwhelming odds.
- A Point of No Return where combatants accept the possibility or inevitability of death.
- Sacrifice for kinship.

## Obligatory Moments — Source-Derived, Structurally Normalized

1. An Inciting Attack or declaration of war.
2. Denial of Responsibility or resistance to involvement.
3. Forced Response according to rank or role.
4. Understanding the antagonist’s object of desire.
5. Failure of the initial strategy.
6. All Is Lost and a change in approach or moral understanding.
7. The Big Battle: courage, strategy, and sacrifice reach maximum pressure.
8. Reward for Sacrifice—or exposure of its corruption—at personal, interpersonal, or extrapersonal levels.

## Conscious Objects of Desire — Source-Derived / Consolidated

- Fulfill or prevent the military objective
- Protect comrades, civilians, homeland, or kin
- Survive or achieve victory without forfeiting honor
- Expose, resist, or obey a command whose moral legitimacy is contested

## Genre Spine

An attack forces reluctant characters into organized conflict. The unit confronts overwhelming odds, rank, duty, and the enemy’s objective. The initial military or moral strategy fails. Kinship deepens as death becomes probable. The Big Battle and its aftermath reveal whether the sacrifice preserves honor and humanity or becomes exploitation, atrocity, betrayal, or meaningless loss.

## Subgenres

### Pro-War

- **Selective constraint / focus:** Conflict is framed as a necessary arena in which honorable defiance and protection can restore dignity.
- **Value rule:** The global controlling-idea Value remains **Honor / Dishonor** unless another genre is explicitly designated global. The subgenre changes the force of antagonism, arena, procedures, and likely Cause.
### Anti-War

- **Selective constraint / focus:** The institutions, illusions, and destruction of war corrode honor; questioning or refusal may be the honorable act.
- **Value rule:** The global controlling-idea Value remains **Honor / Dishonor** unless another genre is explicitly designated global. The subgenre changes the force of antagonism, arena, procedures, and likely Cause.
### Kinship of War

- **Selective constraint / focus:** Comradeship and sacrificial love become the primary measure of honor.
- **Value rule:** The global controlling-idea Value remains **Honor / Dishonor** unless another genre is explicitly designated global. The subgenre changes the force of antagonism, arena, procedures, and likely Cause.

## Adjacent-Genre Disambiguation

- Victory/Defeat and Life/Death are major outcomes but do not replace Honor/Dishonor when War is global.
- Choose Action when a singular hero/villain/victim rescue structure and Life/Death payoff dominate organized conflict and kinship.
- Choose Society when the decisive event is revolution and transfer of Power rather than the moral meaning of battle and sacrifice.

## Controlling-Idea Value Rules

- The controlling idea must resolve Honor or Dishonor.
- Victory, survival, obedience, patriotism, justice, and courage belong in the Cause or secondary charges.
- Military defeat may end in Honor; military victory may end in Dishonor.
- The resolution must show who or what the sacrifice served and whether that service preserved humanity.
