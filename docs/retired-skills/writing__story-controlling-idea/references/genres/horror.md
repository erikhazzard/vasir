## Core Roles — Source-Derived

- **Hero:** A relatable, often initially powerless or ignorant person who faces the horror.
- **Monster / Villain:** The source of fear, often embodying the unknown, taboo, repressed, grotesque, or incomprehensible.
- **Victim:** Those harmed by the horror, establishing what the threat does and what remains at stake.

## Core-Emotion Techniques — Source-Derived

1. Vulnerability in character and setting
2. Suspense and dread through uncertainty
3. Shock, surprise, and sudden threat
4. The grotesque, uncanny, and monstrous
5. Isolation, insanity, and despair

## Genre Conventions — Source-Derived

- An isolated setting or condition that prevents easy help or escape.
- A powerless or ignorant main character.
- A discovery process that progressively reveals the horror.
- An increasing and increasingly personal threat.
- An inescapable situation.
- False security that is shattered.
- Death or a fate worse than death continuously overhanging the characters.

## Obligatory Moments — Source-Derived, Structurally Normalized

1. Discovery of the Horrific.
2. Revelation of the Monster in its horrifying aspect.
3. Increasing Menace that becomes more personal and inescapable.
4. Hero at the Mercy of the Monster.
5. Glimpse of the True Horror and its full implications.
6. Final Confrontation with Evil.
7. False Ending in which safety appears restored and is challenged or reinterpreted.
8. Survival, Death, or Damnation resolution.

## Conscious Objects of Desire — Source-Derived / Consolidated

- Survive and escape
- Understand the source and rules of the horror
- Protect loved ones or innocents
- Destroy, neutralize, contain, or outlast the horror

## Genre Spine

An unsettling anomaly grows through discovery into an inescapable and personal horror. The protagonist’s ordinary explanations and first defenses fail. A revelation exposes the true horror or its rules, forcing a final confrontation in which survival may demand a terrible cost and apparent safety may prove false.

## Twenty-Skeletal-Scene Map — Operational Normalization

The supplied notes often use “climax” for a decision, condition, or encounter. This map enforces:

- **Crisis = choice.**
- **Climax = enacted answer.**
- **Resolution = consequence.**

## Adjacent-Genre Disambiguation

- Choose Thriller when the master villain’s plan, personalized pursuit, clock, unique gift, and restoration of safety dominate over dread and the unknowable.
- Choose Action when excitement, kinetic set pieces, rescue, and the hero’s final tactic dominate over fear and discovery.
- In Psychological Horror, Sanity/Madness may be a major secondary value, but the global controlling idea must still resolve Life/Death/Damnation if Horror is global.

## Controlling-Idea Value Rules

- Fear, truth, sanity, humanity, and self-knowledge may shape the Cause or secondary charges; they may not silently replace the global Horror value.
