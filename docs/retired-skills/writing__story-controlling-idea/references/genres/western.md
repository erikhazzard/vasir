## Value Spectrum — Operational Synthesis

The supplied source names the global poles. The deeper states below operationalize McKee’s contrary, contradictory, and negation-of-negation progression for this genre; tailor the exact wording to the story without leaving the canonical axis.

| Level | Genre state |
|---|---|
| Positive | Freedom and chosen sovereignty |
| Contrary | Constraint, dependence, isolation, or limited agency |
| Contradictory | Subjugation and coercive rule |
| Negation of the negation | Complicity in one’s own subjugation, or tyranny imposed in the name of freedom |

## Core Roles — Source-Derived

- **Hero:** Embodies individual sovereignty and offers the gift unconditionally, often at the cost of solitude or autonomy.
- **Villain:** Embodies subjugation and pursues an object that requires domination of the community.
- **Victim / Community:** Those oppressed or endangered whom the hero must protect.

## Core-Emotion Techniques — Source-Derived

1. Lone hero against oppressive society
2. Autonomy versus conformity
3. Personal desire versus social obligation
4. Justice in an unjust world
5. Frontier as freedom and isolation

## Genre Conventions — Source-Derived

- A harsh, hostile, wide-open landscape symbolizing freedom and isolation.
- A hero who operates outside the law selectively or habitually.
- A large power divide between hero and villain.
- A villain’s MacGuffin or goal that threatens the community.
- A speech or demonstration in praise of the villain.
- Subgenre-specific conventions for vengeance, transition, or professional duty.

## Obligatory Moments — Source-Derived, Structurally Normalized

1. An inciting attack by villain or environment forces engagement.
2. The hero initially sidesteps responsibility to preserve autonomy.
3. Forced from the ordinary world, the hero confronts the threat.
4. The hero’s initial strategy fails.
5. The hero discovers and understands the villain’s MacGuffin.
6. At All Is Lost, the hero needs a new way to protect the community without merely reproducing subjugation.
7. The Showdown tests sovereignty, duty, and sacrifice.
8. The sacrifice is rewarded with ambiguous fulfillment—or refusal/corruption results in Subjugation.

## Conscious Objects of Desire — Source-Derived / Consolidated

- Stop the oppressor’s plan
- Protect the victim or community
- Preserve or redefine personal autonomy
- Restore conditions in which the community can govern itself

## Genre Spine

A sovereign outsider reluctantly engages a community’s oppression. Easy individualistic solutions fail. The hero learns the villain’s true objective and confronts the tension between autonomy and duty. The showdown reveals whether freedom can be protected without becoming domination and what bond, identity, or solitude must be sacrificed.

## Subgenres

### Vengeance

- **Selective constraint / focus:** A town or community is under an oppressor; the hero’s pursuit of vengeance risks reproducing subjugation.
- **Value rule:** The global controlling-idea Value remains **Subjugation / Freedom** unless another genre is explicitly designated global. The subgenre changes the force of antagonism, arena, procedures, and likely Cause.
### Transition

- **Selective constraint / focus:** A community stands at the crossroads of old and new orders; nostalgia, rumor, outsiders, and changing myths shape freedom.
- **Value rule:** The global controlling-idea Value remains **Subjugation / Freedom** unless another genre is explicitly designated global. The subgenre changes the force of antagonism, arena, procedures, and likely Cause.
### Professional

- **Selective constraint / focus:** A seasoned outsider or professional operates in frontier/border conditions and must choose which bonds and duties define sovereignty.
- **Value rule:** The global controlling-idea Value remains **Subjugation / Freedom** unless another genre is explicitly designated global. The subgenre changes the force of antagonism, arena, procedures, and likely Cause.

## Adjacent-Genre Disambiguation

- Justice may motivate the Western hero, but if Western is global the controlling value remains Freedom/Subjugation.
- Choose Society when collective power transfer and revolution—not the sovereign protector’s showdown—control the climax.
- Choose Crime when exposure of a criminal, not liberation of the community, is the indispensable ending payoff.

## Controlling-Idea Value Rules

- The controlling idea must resolve Freedom or Subjugation.
- Justice, revenge, law, honor, community, duty, and self-knowledge belong in the Cause or secondary charges.
- A positive Western must show what form of sovereignty is created, not merely that the villain dies.
- A negative Western must causally show subjugation: the hero becomes tyrant, abandons the community, enables complicity, or destroys the conditions of self-rule.
