# Applying the craft loop across games

Use this reference for broad quality work and ambiguous criticism. These are observation and diagnosis heuristics, not genre requirements or a mandatory capture matrix.

## Choose an interval that can reveal the failure

| This game's repeated experience | A revealing sequence | Important contrast or interruption |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Embodied action or fighting | Intent → commit → contact/miss → recovery → next input | Weak/strong contact, cancel/rebound, occlusion, repeated use |
| Racing, sports or piloting | Accelerate/aim → change direction → grip/contact/release → regain control | Low/high energy, sustained input, moving camera, correction during feedback |
| Cards, tactics or board games | Select/preview → commit → ordered resolution → resulting options → next turn/undo | Invalid choice, multiple consequences, cancel/reselect, hidden/revealed information |
| Puzzle, merge or construction | Inspect → manipulate/place → transformation → stable readable result | Small/large consequence, undo, rapid second input, crowded state |
| Management or idle | Read a bottleneck → make a choice → production/state changes → next decision | Sparse/dense state, elapsed time, repeated small updates versus milestone |
| Rhythm | Read cue → timed input → judgment → next beat | Success/miss, dense passage, strongest accent, latency and recovery |
| Narrative or exploration | Attend/read → choose/inspect → react/reveal → resume | Text advance/skip, speaker/focus change, quiet hold versus consequential reveal |

For hybrids choose the parts that expose the actual promise. An unlisted genre is not a blocker. Reuse its mechanics' observation questions; do not convert it into the closest row.

## Turn broad feedback into a repairable hypothesis

| Feedback | Inspect before choosing a mechanism | A credible improvement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Flat / pasted together” | Shared values/materials, complete contours, overlapping forms, attachment, camera depth, screen hierarchy | Connected forms and clear focal relationships. A flat graphic board may need better grouping, not simulated depth. |
| “Dead / static” | Whether input has a causal answer; whether repeated poses fail to express state; whether stillness serves concentration | Distinct intentional responses or world relationships. Idle motion is optional and must not imply false readiness/progress. |
| “Every action feels the same” | Which events differ in commitment, consequence, scale or material, and whether feedback is already saturated | Ordinary and exceptional events are distinguishable in timing, form, location or sound. A quiet meaningful move can be the strongest accent. |
| “Morph / effect looks fake” | Before, transition, residual material and reentry; connected silhouette; variation constrained by material | An authored transition whose intermediate state and remains belong to the same material, including interruption. |
| “More impact / 10×” | Source of energy/consequence, impact direction, body/object response and next action | A perceptibly stronger appropriate event with a clear contrast to lighter ones, not every event at maximum gain. |
| “Looks good only occasionally” | Ordinary play between highlights, repeated framing, sparse and crowded states, quiet interval quality | A coherent baseline throughout the relevant experience, with changes in emphasis and deliberate room to think. |

The specialist supplies the implementation. A shader is appropriate when it can express a needed material/spatial relation; a simpler geometry, texture, timing or hierarchy repair may produce the missing relation directly. Do not change engine merely because the user requests a higher visual bar.

## Inspect relationships over a whole lifetime

Follow the causal subject: input/choice → acted-on object or information → local consequence → residual state → next agency. The subject may be a physical body or a card's ownership/availability. Check what remains attached, what becomes independent, what persists, and when the player can act again.

A platformer's wing that dissolves into ash and a paper card that folds into a discard pile need different material behavior. A precise chess piece should retain its rigid identity. The transfer lesson is continuity and legibility of the change, not that everything should dissolve or squash.

Atmosphere and ambience need their own spatial/temporal logic where present. A screen-space vignette may correctly stay with the camera; a distant flock should not accidentally behave like HUD decoration. In a still board game, attention can move while the board rests. The designer decides which relationship the player should infer.

## Compare at the surface that matters

Use the target viewport, zoom, input mode and native playback timing. Enlarged frames reveal seams and misleading outlines; they do not tell you whether a brief onset dominates ordinary play. A montage can reveal state differences; it cannot establish input latency or smoothness. For repeated actions, inspect a later sequence after caches, effects and animation state have accumulated.

Keep the previous version until the changed interval is judged. A repaired focal object can make its neighbors look like different materials; a cleanup can erase a hazard's warning strength; a cinematic response can obscure the next legal move. Compare these relationships, not an isolated asset's detail count.

An independent reviewer receives the intended experience and current evidence, not a list of reasons the author believes it is improved. If a comparison is useful, identify the versions neutrally. Ask for the most consequential visible failure and the observed interval supporting it. A reviewer who only read code cannot claim to have watched the motion.

## A useful stopping boundary

Close an observed failure when the relevant experience changes as intended and its immediate aftermath and next action remain sound. Preserve a good accepted baseline when the creator is ready to move on. Separate “this repair works,” “this build is a useful reference,” and “the creator awarded a quality grade.” None automatically implies the others.

If evidence cannot expose the suspected failure, report that uncertainty and choose the smallest faithful next observation. Do not replace the missing observation with more generic polish, a simulated score or repeated approval requests. Once the approved work is concrete and reviewed, deliver it with honest limits.
