# Relational Game Craft

Read when a game feels assembled, flat, static, incoherent, or under-polished despite attractive assets and working controls. These are conditional heuristics across genres. The brief determines the desired material, abstraction, emotional tone, and amount of motion.

Art owns the visible relationships and material/shape grammar below. `$game__adding-juice` owns how responses evolve and interrupt; `$game__orchestrating-playable-build` owns the integrated critique/repair loop. Use the active-play review for the affected visual claim.

## Diagnose the relationship before choosing the effect

| Experienced failure | Inspect first | Repair that changes the read | Boundary / counterexample |
|---|---|---|---|
| “It looks like clip art.” | Adjacent scale, edge finish, silhouette completeness, material roughness, light direction, detail density. | Give neighboring elements compatible visual rules; recompose or replace the mismatched form. | A deliberately illustrated card can differ from its flat frame. Consistency means a legible relationship, not identical treatment everywhere. |
| “It feels flat.” | Occlusion, relative scale, value bands, source alpha, crop, and whether washes erase existing structure. | Establish distinct masses and openings; put the separating treatment between the relevant forms. | A flat puzzle board may need grouping and selection hierarchy, not parallax, fog, or simulated perspective. |
| “This thing is just stuck there.” | Exposed source/cache boundaries; structural continuation; joints at the visible artwork rather than convenient collider centers. | Complete the visible contour, hide a finite boundary behind plausible structure, or author the needed continuation. | Floating geometry can be intentional. Its support rules must agree with that world's established fiction; do not add chains merely to make it realistic. |
| “It still feels like an image.” | Whether the identity-bearing form changes in actual motion; whether new texture is simply carried by a rigid silhouette. | Design a readable change of pose, shape, overlap, or state; then ask juice to realize its material motion. | A chess piece or portrait can remain rigid. Selection, orientation, staging, and the resulting state may carry all needed expression. |
| “There is a burst, but nothing transforms.” | Hide detached effects and inspect the focal body or piece. | Define a distinguishable resulting form/state and preserve an identity anchor into and out of it. | A card upgrade may change rank, frame, or placement; it need not become a creature or explode. |
| “It looks busy but lifeless.” | Are all elements synchronized, camera-bound, or looping without purpose? Is any behavior staged where it can actually be seen? | Select a few readable independent or responsive behaviors with their own timing and depth; protect quiet space. | Stillness can create concentration, suspense, or clarity. Do not animate every tile, UI label, or decorative prop. |
| “The later part looks the same.” | Repeated framing, focal placement, scale relationships, and whether a supposed reveal exposes anything new. | Change composition in relation to the play sequence; preserve place and orientation. | A stable board is often desirable. Variation can come from meaningful state density or resolution, not moving the board around. |
| “It's sharp up close but mushy in play.” | Actual displayed size/DPR, asset-to-cache-to-screen sampling, aspect distortion, tonal compression, and blur. | Preserve useful source information and meaningful value differences before increasing final resolution. | Pixel art may intentionally preserve hard pixels. Smooth scaling and more detail can damage that style. |

## Compose a coherent space—or a coherent screen

For a **spatial game**, separate distances through a combination of scale, overlap, silhouette, contrast, detail, and motion. No required layer count establishes depth. One convincing foreground mass crossing a readable middle form may do more than several pale blurred images. If using fog, light, or shadows, name what emits it, what receives it, and what occludes it. Check existing transparency/draw order before adding masks or render passes. A faint foreground smear is neither recognizable architecture nor useful framing.

For a **flat or information-led game**, the comparable relationship is grouping, ownership, focus, and state. Distinguish a card in hand from a valid target, committed play, and resolved discard; distinguish a placed tile from its preview and an illegal placement. Depth cues are optional. Decorative shading must not make equal pieces look differently actionable.

For either, deliberate occlusion can support mystery or composition; accidental occlusion of the next required decision is a defect. Do not make every element equally contrasty to solve one hidden cue. Assign priority to the decision now: the controlled actor, legal path, target, ball, rhythm cue, selected object, or consequence may lead at different times.

## Material identity survives contact and departure

Specify what makes the material recognizable: silhouette, edge structure, opacity, scale distribution, light response, and association with neighboring forms. Rough ash, brittle stone, flexible cloth, polished metal, paper, and abstract light should not collapse into one universal glossy particle treatment.

The same material can appear as a body, surface, released fragment, or residue. Inspect those appearances together. A smooth leaf-shaped black fragment can begin convincingly at a torn wing yet cease to read as ash during its aftermath. Fading an intact polygon does not necessarily communicate crumbling. Art identifies that mismatch; juice owns the breakup, drift, settling, and interruption behavior that repairs it.

When stronger feedback exposes wrong material, preserve the successfully requested strength and repair the substance. Conversely, reducing a hazard's bright outline to unify the palette must retain its warning role. “More coherent” is not an improvement if it hides danger, legality, ownership, or a scoring cue.

## Designed life and ordinary craft

Spend effort where the player actually spends time. A beautifully staged special cannot carry generic ordinary actions, flat intervening compositions, or a poor return to control. Select the ordinary state transitions that matter for the brief. If there is an actor, use attention and follow-through where they express intention; if there is no actor, invest in the manipulated pieces, focus transitions, or readable system consequences.

Stage a behavior so a player can see it during normal play. A responsive creature hidden in dark stone is a composition failure even if its eyes and limbs animate correctly. In another game, a valid-target reaction concealed by the dragged card has the same failure. Moving it into view may solve more than increasing amplitude.

Independent life should have a local cause and a meaningful relationship to scale. Small contact may disturb nearby dust, a suspended object, or spectators near a collision. It should not accidentally drag remote atmosphere, the whole crowd, or unrelated board UI with it. Use the juice specialist for the causal response. Ambient drift can continue independently; quiet states can remain still.

## Cross-genre contrastive examples

| Game / decision | A crafted direction | Tempting but wrong transfer |
|---|---|---|
| Painted exploration / traverse a ruin | Complete connected stone, recognizable near/middle/far masses, selective air between forms, living material on the actor. | Mandatory fog layers, Gothic architecture, a raven morph, or endless particle escalation. |
| Deckbuilder / choose and resolve a card | Stable hand hierarchy; clear targeting and commitment; material-consistent cards; a combo reveals its causal order and leaves a legible new state. | Shaking the reading surface, making every card idle-wiggle, or adding an avatar so “character juice” has somewhere to go. |
| Abstract placement puzzle / preview, commit, undo | Crisp legal-space grammar; deliberate quiet during thought; decisive commitment and faithful restoration on undo. | Squash that changes apparent cell occupancy, permanent ambient movement, or bursts hiding the move that scored. |
| Racing or sports / turn, collide, recover | Track/field and participant hierarchy; material-specific contact; vehicle or athlete poses that show load; effects leave the next line or play readable. | Applying platformer jump poses or letting an oversized trail imply a different vehicle width. |
| Rhythm / read the next cue and hit timing | Predictable timing landmarks, clean approach lanes, authored accents subordinate to the next cue. | Camera shake or postprocessing that displaces the timing reference during rapid input. |
| Strategy or management / inspect then order | Stable grouping and ownership; selected command and resource consequence have distinct focus; background life stays below decision data. | Fog/parallax added to make charts and tiles “deeper,” or spectacle that hides simulation state. |
| Narrative or social / listen and choose | Composition, gaze, typography, and selective response express relationships; silence and held poses support the scene. | Constant looping expression, combat-style hit effects on dialogue, or requiring a visible threat in every active moment. |

These examples test whether the general judgment transfers. They do not establish that one game's accepted visual bar or one agent's favorable review proves quality across genres.
