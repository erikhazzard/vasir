# Art-direction foundations

Read when choosing or explaining the basis for a visual grammar. This is a source map, not a requirement to imitate a named work. The game applications below are this skill's synthesis; they are not claims that every author prescribes these exact rules.

## Composition and hierarchy

- Bruce Block, *The Visual Story*: space, line, shape, tone, color, movement, and rhythm can be designed as coupled visual components. The game-art application is to control contrast across the whole moving frame instead of polishing assets independently.
- Marcos Mateu-Mestre, *Framed Ink*: value grouping, staging, and eye flow determine what the viewer reads first. In games, direct that path toward the current decision and its consequence.
- James Gurney, *Color and Light*: local color, light color, atmosphere, value grouping, and material response interact. Judge a palette under its intended lighting and viewing distance.
- Josef Albers, *Interaction of Color*: colors are relational. Judge semantic colors in their actual adjacency, frequency, and value context, not only as isolated swatches.

## Shape, world building, and production grammar

- Patrick Faulwetter, [Art Direction Bootcamp: Building Worlds Through Shape Language](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025897/Art-Direction-Bootcamp-Building-Worlds): connect a shape language to the world's culture, architecture, costume, transport, and environment rather than designing isolated props.
- Geneviève Routhier, [Building a Visual Identity: An Art Direction Framework](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1028954/Art-Direction-Summit-Building-a): define visual identity across artistic, creative, technical, and marketing considerations; appealing key art alone does not establish the framework.
- Khang Le, Mike Yamada, and Felix Yoon, guided by Scott Robertson, [The Skillful Huntsman](https://designstudiopress.com/products/the-skillful-huntsman): a documented concept-design process spanning environments, characters, props, and vehicles. Use it to examine how construction, proportion, and shape decisions can connect an asset family beyond surface resemblance.
- Valve, [Illustrative Rendering in Team Fortress 2](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2007/NPAR07_IllustrativeRenderingInTeamFortress2.pdf): illustrative rendering couples asset design and shading to gameplay readability. Choose material, lighting, and value relationships for a visual target rather than letting a shader technique define the style by itself.
- James Golding, Epic Games, [Building Blocks: Artist Driven Procedural Buildings](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1012219/Building-Blocks-Artist-Driven-Procedural): artist-authored meshes and rules guide procedural construction. Define the grammar before asking a generator to recombine it.

## Motion and character

- Frank Thomas and Ollie Johnston, *The Illusion of Life*: anticipation, staging, timing, arcs, exaggeration, and follow-through make actions readable and intentional.
- Richard Williams, *The Animator's Survival Kit*: spacing and timing communicate weight and personality. More frames alone do not establish either.

Use these principles to specify the intended expression. `$game__adding-juice` owns response timing, interruption, and material-motion implementation.

## Game-specific interpretation

Apply these as conditional design heuristics:

1. Design the moving frame or active decision surface, not isolated assets.
2. Resolve hierarchy, silhouette, proportion, and value before adding detail.
3. Treat color, material, lighting, motion, and composition as interacting systems.
4. Let procedural tools arrange an authored grammar rather than inventing the grammar.
5. Judge style in the actual play moment and at the target viewport.

These sources do not establish universal numeric budgets, a mandatory aesthetic, or permission to copy recognizable proprietary assets. A flat board can express a coherent grammar through grouping, typography, and state without simulated lighting or character animation.
