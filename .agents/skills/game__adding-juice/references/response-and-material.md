# Response Through Time

Read when the request concerns a special action, transformation, impact range, trail, responsive environment or repeated-action mix. These are cross-genre diagnostic heuristics; choose the examples that fit the game's actual fantasy, rules and display. Art direction owns the visual/material language, and the playable-build orchestrator owns the integrated review record.

## Follow one complete action

Ask what the player notices without implementation labels:

1. **Intent and acknowledgment:** does the focus acknowledge the actual choice before its consequence, without suggesting a different choice or adding unauthorized delay?
2. **Commitment and consequence:** is the state change distinguishable from hovering, previewing, failing, cancelling or repeating the ordinary action?
3. **Aftermath:** does the receiver or released material explain where force/value went after the focus moved away?
4. **Next action:** can the player act and read the new state while compatible aftermath continues? Does repeated input produce stale poses, duplicate sound attacks or an unreadable accumulating cloud?

Do not require every event to have four animation phases. The phrase is an inspection window. A chess move may acknowledge selection, settle the piece, preserve a last-move marker and immediately expose legal next interaction. An elaborate wind-up before that move can make the board worse.

## State and consequence across genres

| Game / focus | Distinction worth making | Useful performance and aftermath | Tempting wrong repair |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calm spatial puzzle / selected tile and receiving group | Hover preview, legal placement, rejected placement, new relationship, undo | A stable preview; crisp acceptance; a brief contour path along newly connected cells; persistent changed state; immediate selection or undo | Add impact shake and confetti to every placement, concealing the board the player needs to study |
| Turn-based cards / hand, card and target | Pick up, target, commit, resolve, cancel | Card leads intent while neighboring cards make space; resolution travels to the actual recipient; spend/availability remains clear after flourish | Bend every card like jelly, delay the next decision, or make ordinary play and rare combination share one larger scale punch |
| Racing / car, tires and road | Steering intent, grip, slip, braking, boost, collision | Chassis loads and settles; tire contact leaves appropriate spray/skid; independent spray remains on the road; boost has a distinct delivery and ending | Stretch the car to imply speed, make every skid shake the camera, or tether tire smoke to the car after release |
| Combat / weapon, body and target | Wind-up, committed strike, armor deflection, stagger, charged hit | Source recoil and receiver reaction agree in direction; distinct material contact; charged action has an authored gesture; next target remains visible | Give every hit the same flash/stop/shake stack or let cosmetic animation change damage timing |
| Strategy or management / selected region, transfer and destination | Pending order, execution, arrival, capacity failure | Local work starts at the issuer, transfer arrives at the receiver, quantities and availability update clearly; background production follows its own rhythm | A board-wide pulse on every resource tick, arbitrary idle motion, or celebration that hides a still-failing order |

None of these examples requires an avatar, particles, fog, shaders or spatial camera. Translate the quality of response, not the platformer's effects.

## Make a different action read as different

Two parameter sets can occupy the same perceptual category. If normal and special actions both show the same subject and narrow trail, extra noise or a brighter burst may still read as repetition. Change the feature that defines the action: a weapon's charge release, a vehicle's traction-to-boost transition, a tile group's connection/reconfiguration, or a creature's connected anatomy. Keep ordinary actions satisfying; an exceptional move must not carry all the game's craft.

For an actual morph, temporarily hide detached effects. Identify the temporary form, the stable identity anchor, the order of articulation and the return. Follow near and far masses through the action, with uneven loading and secondary lag where the style warrants it. Then restore effects and inspect the full sequence. A readable bird-shaped peak can still be a static stamp; a shader changing pixels can still leave its anatomy inert. Conversely, a rigid geometric token may be correct—the meaningful transformation can be its relationship to the board.

Recovery belongs to the transformation. Shrinking the entire subject with one scalar often skips the material story: limbs/edges, core and released matter all disappear together. Stage reassembly, folding or disintegration so the subject becomes itself again while identity and next-action timing remain coherent. Interrupt an early and late phase; abrupt contact, cancellation, retry or new intent should not expose a stale transformed body.

## Preserve the event range the player experiences

Start with common actions in the real game, then its actual extremes on comparable recipients. Trace the source signal through consumers: velocity may already be reset at contact, terminal speed can collapse long falls into one value, damage may be capped, and several downstream clamps can erase a useful range. A reported event name or nominal maximum says little about displayed response.

Choose the metric for the meaning being communicated. A racing collision might use normal closing speed and contact material; slip feedback uses tire slip. A reward response may use strategic importance and rarity rather than raw currency magnitude. An ordinary puzzle placement can be quiet while a completed relationship unfolds across only its affected cells. A platformer may require descent history as well as pre-impact velocity when long falls should remain distinguishable beyond terminal speed.

Use a shared semantic signal where several channels describe the same event, but let each spend it differently. Shape, onset, spatial reach, duration, pitch, connected response and aftermath can vary independently; multiplying every amplitude usually compresses the difference or causes clutter. Judge the common middle of the range, not just a tiny tap against an improbable showcase event. When the upper range still looks identical, consider a distinct high-consequence sequence rather than another small amplitude increase.

Preserve accepted intensity through repair. If a powerful contact looks like cardboard, fix its material lifetime before reducing its force. If the next action is obscured, locate the competing layer and its lifetime before weakening the entire response. A calm game's strongest event can still keep the camera still.

## Material must remain itself after release

| Intended material / signal | Evolution to inspect | Failure read |
|---|---|---|
| Ash / soot | Brief coherent tear → unequal pieces → porous erosion and fine drift, with some dense grains surviving | Smooth leaves, a rigid comet appendage, orbiting rocks or whole polygons fading unchanged |
| Stone | Fracture at a plausible face → a few weighty chips → finer dust and settling | Uniform confetti or identical weightless pieces |
| Water / spray | Contact sheet or beads → breakup, drag and gravity → droplets or wake | A fixed stretched splash carried by the source |
| Paper / cards | Art-directed stiffness, curl and edge behavior; ordered placement when resolving | Liquid rubber motion that contradicts a crisp tactile deck |
| Electronic / abstract confirmation | Directed transfer or clean local state change with a deliberate end | Unrelated debris, ambient shimmer or a persistent glow that looks like another selectable state |

Controlled variation changes enough dimensions to avoid a repeated stamp—size distribution, timing, initial direction, drift, porosity and lifetime—while protecting the gesture and identity. Randomizing everything every frame creates shimmer rather than material. A few coherent masses can establish force; changing their scale and structure afterward can establish dispersal. Always judge the full lifetime at normal cadence. Slow motion or a selected peak diagnoses a moment but cannot certify the perceived material.

## Causality and independent life

Show the contact or transfer at its real origin, then what receives it. A wall kick can leave a strike at the wall after the body leaves; a coin enters the actual total; a car's tire plume originates at the road contact. A cause hidden under the subject or foreground may need staging, a readable leading edge or better contrast rather than more particles.

Connected elements respond according to material and delay: load → support motion → cable tension → hanging object follow-through. The response must be large and long enough to register at target size, yet belong to the material. Decorative springs are optional. If a support actually moves riders, its displacement is gameplay and must pass through collision/constraints; a beautiful sway that carries a body through a wall is a failed response.

Keep unrelated ambient motion independent from input and camera. Weather, wildlife, cloth, background machinery or idle production can have their own bounded rhythm. A tiny step should not reshape cathedral-scale fog; dragging one tile should not wobble the entire board. Use local influence where plausible, and leave stillness where it gives attention room. If the only visible ambient movement occurs when the camera moves, inspect baked scenery, coordinate choice and parallax coupling before adding more actors.

## Diagnose the actual defect

Use the cheapest comparison that separates causes, then return to unmodified play:

- **Looks static:** inspect connected anatomy/geometry with emissions hidden, then its articulation at normal speed. If that moves, isolate material flow from object transport.
- **Feels weak:** inspect displayed size/duration, occlusion, relevant settings and common event values. Compare onset while the player immediately continues.
- **Wrong substance:** watch attached form → departure → late remnants. Isolate flat geometry/mask defects before compensating with lighting; winding errors and exposed cache edges can masquerade as material problems.
- **Overwhelming:** replay rapid inputs. Find the layer that outlives its useful role, duplicates another effect or hides the next decision. Retain the event's meaningful force elsewhere.
- **Looks detached:** follow the source/receiver coordinates, local light, attachment and support motion. A correct isolated effect can fail in composition.

Keep arranged setups, modified diagnostic views and real played sequences distinct in the existing work record. The final judgment needs the integrated candidate at ordinary play scale, with input and state intact.
