# Source Notes and Decision Anchors

Primary source: [Technical Artist Bootcamp: The VFX of Diablo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YPy2hytwDLM), Julian Love, Blizzard Entertainment, GDC 2013. [Official session record](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1017660/Technical-Artist-Bootcamp-The-VFX).

The source was reviewed through its full automatic captions and sampled recording frames, including detailed formula and example slides. Notes are paraphrases, not a verbatim transcript. Timestamps refer to the YouTube recording. The following distinctions preserve the talk's judgment without treating every historical production choice as a rule.

| Time | Source observation | Decision carried into the skill |
|---|---|---|
| [01:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YPy2hytwDLM&t=99s) | Four priorities connect gameplay communication, relatability, readability, and sustained visual interest. | Judge techniques against the player's understanding and the effect's intended experience. |
| [02:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YPy2hytwDLM&t=171s) | A large visible blast with a smaller damage area forces players to compensate for misleading graphics. The team compares effects with payload overlays. | Check visible coverage against mechanics. The anecdote's inconsistent yardages are not a measured ratio. |
| [04:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YPy2hytwDLM&t=291s) | Designers intended an early-game Ballista; artists imagined an enormous magical attack. The spectacle promised excessive power. | Agree on the event and consequence before asset construction; adjust presentation rather than secretly changing balance. |
| [06:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YPy2hytwDLM&t=387s) | Frozen showed where damage had happened. A patch added information about where it would happen, accepting a more overtly game-like appearance. | Prioritize information while the player can still act on it. |
| [07:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YPy2hytwDLM&t=475s) | Early Earthquake had pointed forms but soft, insufficiently destructive motion. The revision increased activity. | Diagnose material motion and intensity before increasing density. |
| [09:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YPy2hytwDLM&t=552s) | A blue ground hazard looks placid and merges with its setting. Desecrator uses fire/lava, flashes, and outward motion. | Use recognizable danger cues with sufficient local contrast; do not infer that visual design alone caused the shown raid failure. |
| [11:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YPy2hytwDLM&t=680s) | Chris Haga's heartbeat comparison helps discuss attractive versus repellent rhythms. | Use tempo as a contextual art-direction heuristic, not a scientific threshold. |
| [12:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YPy2hytwDLM&t=746s) | Damaging blood has fewer familiar cues because blood commonly follows injury. References include a helicopter crash, carpet bombing for Seismic Slam, eating a candy bar, and a Jaws license-plate joke. | Borrow recognizable action and causality; the reference need not literally match the fictional mechanism. |
| [16:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YPy2hytwDLM&t=971s) | Attractive arbitrary shapes can lack meaning. Crippling Wave lacked a strong visual concept; level-up is intrinsically abstract. | Give abstract effects a readable vocabulary rather than banning geometric or abstract art. |
| [18:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YPy2hytwDLM&t=1093s) | Students often begin with a texture or shader before describing the event. A demonstration makes dust turn into locusts. | Describe origin and unfolding action before choosing the rendering technique. |
| [19:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YPy2hytwDLM&t=1183s) | Additive lightning loses shape and color on bright backgrounds. Blend-add uses alpha to vary blending versus addition. | Preserve both luminosity and recognition; test against relevant backgrounds and overlapping abilities. |
| [23:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YPy2hytwDLM&t=1389s) | Campfires remain interesting through evolving motion. Recognizable flipbook repeats can weaken that interest. | Evaluate lingering effects over time, not just at their strongest frame. |
| [25:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YPy2hytwDLM&t=1538s) | Multiplication of independently scrolling cloud samples becomes richer with different frequencies and a shape mask. | Try internal shape evolution before buying complexity with particle count. |
| [28:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YPy2hytwDLM&t=1698s) | Poison, fire, sand, frost, and magic demonstrate reuse of the approach. Color multiplication adds internal motion. | Compare the perceptual contribution of each layer at gameplay scale. |
| [31:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YPy2hytwDLM&t=1882s) | Painted light and dynamically changing alpha produce thick-looking smoke without a volume simulation. Fire uses similar layered sampling. | Preserve the useful illusion within its camera/light limits and check transparent coverage. |
| [34:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YPy2hytwDLM&t=2052s) | The recap reconnects techniques to communication, relatability, readability, and engaging motion. | Keep the design purpose visible while tuning shaders. |

## Q&A qualifications

- [35:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YPy2hytwDLM&t=2154s): Most source work used Photoshop, with substantial painting. Love estimates roughly 80% painting in his own work. This supports deliberate stylization; it is not a required production ratio or a ban on simulation.
- [37:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YPy2hytwDLM&t=2251s): Playtesting and knowledge of the game's effect vocabulary help resolve unpredictable overlaps. Blue fire may create ambiguity about damage type. Arcane Sentry's earlier Hydra-like presentation motivated a collaborative ability redesign, not a unilateral graphics change to gameplay.
- [39:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YPy2hytwDLM&t=2377s): Love considers the arithmetic mobile-feasible and identifies per-particle tracking of scrolling textures as a significant CPU cost in their implementation. He persuaded programmers to support additional layers by promising lower particle counts. This is a historical tradeoff, not a diagnosis of a modern engine.
- [40:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YPy2hytwDLM&t=2459s): VFX artists and combat designers sit together and exchange ideas. The Witch Doctor's exploding chicken exemplifies a visual idea influencing design through collaboration.
- [42:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YPy2hytwDLM&t=2537s): Transparent content is sorted together, with ordering offsets per system/geometry. Blend-add remains one pass. Love does not know whether UV transformations happen in the vertex or pixel shader.
- [44:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YPy2hytwDLM&t=2661s): Small separate textures support their scrolling and reuse strategy; a modified cloud texture is reused widely. This does not establish that atlasing is always wrong or that every effect should use 128/256 textures.
- [45:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YPy2hytwDLM&t=2736s): Flipbooks remain useful for flicker, shape choices, and distant crowds such as Act III's background battle. Love's objections elsewhere concern memory, recognizable cadence, repetition, and style. Modern interpolation and a short viewing duration can change that tradeoff.

## Provenance boundaries

The shader reference preserves the displayed formulas and particle counts. The fire-wall slide says 28 particles; the later spoken example says about 25. Love's informal estimate of a 10–20% artistic improvement to Arcane Orb is not a measured quality or performance result. Four sampled layers in the cloud example reuse only two unique source textures; other effects have different inputs.

The root skill's lifecycle, quality-tier, replay, accessibility, renderer-integration, and measurement instructions are present-day application guidance tied to the host project's contracts. They are not quotations or claims that this talk specified those systems. The talk supplies no universal performance budget, complete blend-state implementation, or mathematically nonrepeating animation guarantee.
