# Source analysis, aesthetic references, and evaluation

## Analyze the supplied prose

Read passages long enough to understand their context, paragraph movement, and the function of local choices. Sample the actual range of the relevant material, including surrounding and connecting prose where it occurs. Do not assume that every author alternates plain language with expressive peaks or that the most memorable lines are representative.

Retain source identity and stable locations in the analysis, such as file offsets, chapter plus paragraph ranges, or edition-specific pages. Keep quoted evidence accurate, label omissions, and check extraction artifacts when they could affect an observation. Treat an existing analysis as a hypothesis to verify; state when evidence is limited or a requested mode is absent.

Distinguish author-level observations from a particular narrator, character, subject, or scene. An analysis may describe distinctive features and compare existing prose, including answering whether a sentence fits a particular book, with evidence and uncertainty. This critical comparison is not a recipe for recreating a living author's voice. Keep judge-only passages and reference answers out of writer inputs. Source reading may be an explicit condition of a permitted experiment; document that exposure rather than silently replacing it with a distillation.

## Keep analysis and composition inputs distinct

Select a few general craft choices relevant to the new piece. Describe them in ordinary language, linking each to a practical purpose. Possibilities include varied sentence length for emphasis, concrete detail for legibility, reflective distance, dialogue density, or paragraph development. None is required, and none should encode a source's exact measurements, signature rhythms, recurring metaphors, or distinctive verbal habits.

Keep three things clear in a source-to-craft handoff: what the new piece must accomplish, which shared craft choices to explore, and which creative choices remain independent. Do not use source quotations as composition templates or the author's identity as a target voice. A detailed analysis of a living author's distinctive habits stays an analytical record; renaming that fingerprint does not turn it into an original aesthetic. Let the new content determine imagery, wording, structure, and voice.

For analysis-only tasks, return the analysis and requested source evidence. For writing tasks, use shared craft guidance or an independently developed aesthetic reference. Preserve the fuller analysis separately when the user wants a reviewable record.

## Build and apply an original aesthetic reference

A reference should make an aesthetic usable, including its tensions and costs. It can be substantial. Do not reduce an independently supplied reference to a tiny generic trait list or discard its examples and qualifications. Read the full reference and pass it to the drafting or revision instruction.

A useful reference gives the reader:

- **Aesthetic aim:** the experience the prose should offer, in concrete terms.
- **Priority order:** what wins when beauty, clarity, pace, candor, surprise, and other aims compete; distinguish user constraints from aesthetic preferences.
- **Conditional choices:** how diction, syntax, rhythm, imagery, distance, and paragraph movement can serve that aim, including when a preferred choice fails.
- **Relevant forms:** how the same priorities change in a maxim, exchange, description, scene, or other requested form. Do not make one form the default for every task.
- **Original worked examples:** compare versions with the same content, explain the gain and the cost, and include enough connected prose to show relationships beyond the sentence. Examples illustrate choices, not reusable verbal templates.
- **Failure tests:** identify attractive but empty phrasing, missing substance, changed meaning, disproportionate emphasis, and other defects specific to the aesthetic. Include cases where leaving a plainer original alone is the better revision.

This is a design aid, not a mandatory table of contents. Each reference may organize its own priorities. It should contain enough positive demonstration to make the desired effect tangible; a long list of prohibitions is insufficient. Likewise, qualifications should distinguish where a choice works, rather than weakening every sentence by default. Test the engine with a substantially different reference to detect preferences accidentally hardcoded into the workflow.

## Preserve the requested experiment

For a small exploratory trial, start with the simplest permitted test the user requested. Preserve the requested wording separately from the complete executed input, including wrappers and added instructions. Do not automatically add the production skill, full craft manual, examples, an editing pass, or numerical judges. These are experimental conditions when requested, not free improvements to a control.

Preserve discretionary choices. A request for a short exchange does not by itself require a fixed turn count, speaker labels, or dialogue without narration. Equally, a successful narrated example does not make narration mandatory. Add form restrictions only when the request or an actual operational limit requires them, and record the reason.

When source exposure is part of the permitted test, supply the agreed material. Record the file, exact selected coverage, truncation or compaction, model settings, and available usage. A full book, a source prefix, close-reading notes, and a distilled reference are different inputs. Disclose a necessary reduction before generation; do not describe a partial reading as the full book.

If the requested experiment cannot be run as specified, explain the actual limitation before spending a generation on a different test. Do not quietly insert an opposing objective or imply that renaming a request removes a governing restriction. For permitted original-writing tasks, describe the desired craft positively and leave independent artistic choices open.

Preserve the first result unchanged and attach the user's actual response. A small trial ends with that result unless further work was requested. Evaluate the artistic effect and practical coherence as well as execution compliance. For an external example, retain the user-reported prompt and output, label unknown settings, and distinguish preference from causal evidence. Different models, prompts, forms, source coverage, or selection histories prevent attributing the gap to one instruction. A successful example can justify further investigation without establishing a benchmark gain.

## Evaluate the stated task

For ordinary writing, review the task and full selected reference. Keep style adherence, form and constraint compliance, writing quality, and originality separate. Source resemblance may be described analytically when requested, but it is not a generation or revision objective. Do not optimize creative output toward an author-recognition score.

For controlled comparisons, freeze the task, reference, prompts, model settings, and evaluation procedure before generation. Retain first outputs, revisions, errors, and retries with their exact inputs. Compare reference guidance under otherwise matched conditions, and keep the effect of that guidance separate from the effect of an additional editing pass. Record actual tool reads when fresh agents can discover additional instructions; a new conversation alone does not prove isolated inputs.

Select test forms that exercise the requested qualities. First record the intended form, register, and effect for each brief; then choose its subject. If the target includes concentrated wit and sustained lyricism, an evaluation consisting entirely of everyday explanatory scenes leaves those capabilities untested. Evaluate short forms and longer passages separately rather than hiding a weak form in one average. Use unrelated subjects to check transfer only when the requested effects still have room to operate.

Give evaluators the task, constraints, and relevant aesthetic priorities. Assess adherence, reading quality, factual and causal compliance, and originality separately, with specific evidence and uncertainty. Neutral condition labels and randomized order can reduce avoidable cues. Do not quietly add author resemblance to the success criteria. For qualitative editorial reviews, preserve both successful passages and concrete weaknesses; do not infer an improvement percentage without a suitable comparison.

Ask judges whether the language achieves a worthwhile effect, not merely whether a device appears. Repetition, brevity, or musical phrasing can be present in weak prose. Require a concrete explanation of what the writing makes clearer, sharper, more memorable, or more affecting, together with its weakest passage. If using a 9/10 threshold, define it as exceptional execution of this declared craft task with only minor weaknesses, and report individual scores and confidence. A high craft score is not a probability of authorship or evidence that a passage belongs in a particular novel.

If conducting a separate analysis of resemblance in existing prose, keep judge references separate from any analysis excerpts used to describe the source. Authentic-source calibration can reveal limits or inconsistency in those judgments; it does not establish a creative target or numerical ceiling. Do not feed those judgments back into creative rewriting.

Preserve frozen artifacts and raw judgments when the skill changes. Historical results apply to the actual version tested; they do not validate new instructions. Version subsequent tests and use fresh tasks when assessing a revision instead of repeatedly adapting the same outputs to evaluator feedback.
