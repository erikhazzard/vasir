# Genre Index and Selection Rules

## Non-Negotiable Value Map

| Global genre | Mandatory controlling-idea value axis | Core event | Primary conscious pursuit | Core emotion | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Action | **Life / Death** | Mercy Scene: hero at the mercy of the villain turns the tables | Stop villain; save victim; prevent disaster | Excitement | `genres/action.md` |
| Horror | **Life / Death**, deepening to **Damnation / fate worse than death** | Confrontation with Evil | Survive, understand, protect, neutralize horror | Fear | `genres/horror.md` |
| Crime | **Justice / Injustice** | Exposure of the Criminal | Solve crime; expose criminal; bring to justice or escape it | Intrigue / curiosity | `genres/crime.md` |
| Love | **Love / Hate** or **Intimacy / Estrangement** | Proof of Love | Form or preserve a committed intimate relationship | Connection / romance | `genres/love.md` |
| Performance | **Shame / Respect** | Big Performance | Express gift; earn respect; attain mastery | Triumph | `genres/performance.md` |
| Western | **Subjugation / Freedom** | Big Showdown | Protect community/victim while preserving or redefining sovereignty | Intrigue | `genres/western.md` |
| Society | **Impotence / Power** | Revolution: power changes hands | Gain recognition/power; alter or overthrow hierarchy | Intrigue | `genres/society.md` |
| Thriller | **Damnation / Life** | Hero at the Mercy of the Villain | Restore personal and social safety by stopping master villain | Excitement | `genres/thriller.md` |
| War | **Honor / Dishonor** | Big Battle and sacrificial reckoning | Fulfill mission, protect kin, survive or prevail without losing honor | Intrigue | `genres/war.md` |

## Selection Principle

Select global genre by the **story-long object of desire and final core event**, not by setting, iconography, profession, or tone.

- A story set in the Old West is not automatically a Western. If its final payoff is exposing a murderer, it may be Crime.
- A boxing story is not automatically Performance. If the decisive payoff is survival against a murderous opponent, it may be Action or Thriller.
- A political story is not automatically Society. If its final payoff is exposing a criminal conspiracy, Crime or Thriller may be global.
- A romance containing murder is not automatically Crime. Ask which object of desire and core event control the global climax.

## Disambiguation

### Action vs. Thriller vs. Horror

All can involve life and death.

- **Action:** excitement from kinetic danger; hero/villain/victim; rescue or defeat; Mercy Scene; altruistic sacrifice.
- **Thriller:** safety threatened by a master villain; protagonist becomes the victim; investigation, clock, personalization, unique gift, false ending; damnation is possible.
- **Horror:** fear and dread; powerless or ignorant protagonist; isolation, discovery, confrontation with evil; death may deepen into possession, insanity, eternal suffering, or loss of identity.

### Crime vs. Thriller

- **Crime:** audience promise is exposure; global value is Justice/Injustice. The criminal may escape punishment and the Crime story can still pay off if exposure occurs.
- **Thriller:** audience promise is safety under extreme threat; global value is Life/Damnation. Investigation is a means to stop the master villain.

### Western vs. Society

- **Western:** individual sovereignty tested against duty to protect a community; global value is Freedom/Subjugation; core event is the showdown.
- **Society:** collective hierarchy and recognition; global value is Power/Impotence; core event is a revolution where control changes hands.

### Action vs. War

- **Action:** global question is whether life is preserved or lost through heroism against a villainous force.
- **War:** global question is whether honor is upheld or destroyed amid organized conflict and sacrifice. Military victory and the War controlling idea’s value charge may diverge.

### Love vs. Obsession, Thriller, or Horror

If the final proof concerns genuine intimacy, Love is global. If the final core event is survival from an obsessive pursuer or monstrous beloved, Thriller or Horror may be global and Love secondary.

### Performance vs. Status or Success

The supplied Performance genre value is **Shame/Respect**, not success/failure. Winning, fame, money, mastery, and applause are possible causes or consequences; they do not replace the global value.

## Hybrid Rule

1. Name every active genre.
2. Choose exactly one global genre.
3. Load all relevant files.
4. The global genre supplies the controlling idea’s Value.
5. Secondary genres supply subplot values, complications, mixed ending charges, and possible causal mechanisms.
6. The final climax must pay off the global genre’s core event. A secondary genre may not silently take over.

## Value-Lock Examples

| Story classification | Invalid controlling idea | Why invalid | Valid form |
|---|---|---|---|
| Action | “Freedom is achieved through courage.” | Freedom is Western’s global value, not Action’s. | “Life is preserved when the captive hero sacrifices escape to save the victims.” |
| Crime | “Truth triumphs through investigation.” | Truth is a means; Crime resolves Justice/Injustice. | “Justice is restored when the detective exposes the evidence their own institution buried.” |
| Society | “Justice prevails when the rebels unite.” | Society resolves Power/Impotence. | “Power passes to the disenfranchised when rivals form a coalition that no faction can dominate alone.” |
| War | “Victory is achieved through sacrifice.” | Victory is an outcome; War resolves Honor/Dishonor. | “Honor is preserved when soldiers refuse a winning order that requires murdering civilians.” |
| Performance | “Success is earned through authenticity.” | Performance resolves Shame/Respect. | “Respect is earned when the performer risks public failure to express the gift without imitation.” |
| Psychological Horror | “Sanity returns through shadow integration.” | Sanity may be secondary; Horror’s global value is Life/Death/Damnation. | “Life is reclaimed when the victim names and confronts the presence that survives by making her distrust her own perception.” |
