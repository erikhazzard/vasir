# Philosophy and Religious Source Protocol

## Purpose

Philosophy deepens the **Cause**, counter-idea, crisis, and consequences. It never supplies a replacement for the global genre’s mandatory Value.

## Source Standard

Before naming a thinker, school, text, or religious tradition, use one of:

1. A primary text.
2. A recognized scholarly edition or translation.
3. A peer-reviewed academic source.
4. A reputable academic reference work.
5. An official or authoritative source for a religious tradition.

Do not use quote aggregators, unsourced social posts, generic listicles, or a podcast summary as final authority.

## Research Sequence

1. Identify the human problem already present in the story.
2. Search for traditions that address that exact problem.
3. Read the relevant primary or academic passage in context.
4. State the proposition in plain language.
5. Distinguish the source’s claim from your narrative inference.
6. Find the strongest serious counter-position.
7. Translate both positions into character behavior.
8. Test them through crisis, climax, and consequence.
9. Cite the source when the output format supports citations.

## Fidelity Rules

Reject or revise when:

- The same generic insight could be attributed to ten unrelated traditions.
- The source is metaphysical or epistemological but the output invents an ethical command without argument.
- The philosophy field can be deleted without changing character action or the ending.
- A contested school is reduced to a slogan.
- A thinker’s name is attached after the story idea is complete.
- The attribution is uncertain but presented as fact.

Use “resonates with” when the relationship is interpretive. Use “draws directly on” only when the story mechanism accurately instantiates the source proposition.

## When Source Access Is Unavailable

- Use only propositions already present in trusted supplied references or known with high confidence.
- Prefer a broad accurate tradition over an obscure uncertain attribution.
- Do not invent quotations, citations, titles, or doctrinal claims.
- Mark the foundation provisional or omit the name and state the philosophical tension directly.

## Plain-Language Rule

Reason at full complexity first. Simplify last.

Elementary reading level means:

- Clear nouns and verbs.
- Short causal sentence.
- No unnecessary jargon.

It does not mean removing agency, mechanism, cost, conditions, or opposition.
