## Core Roles — Source-Derived

- **Protagonist:** An individual whose unique gift can influence the world and counter the master villain.
- **Master Villain:** Commits a signature crime against society, has a dangerous MacGuffin, and personalizes the conflict.
- **Victim:** Embodies the wider human cost and may include the protagonist after the threat becomes personal.

## Core-Emotion Techniques — Source-Derived

2. Investigation and red herrings
3. Personalization of threat
4. Clock and deadline pressure
5. False endings and reversals

## Genre Spine

A master villain commits a signature crime. Investigation reveals the magnitude of the plan while red herrings and personalization convert the protagonist into the victim. The initial strategy fails. At the final confrontation, the hero is at the villain’s mercy and must use the unique gift in a new way. A false ending tests whether Life or Damnation is truly final.

## Adjacent-Genre Disambiguation

- Choose Crime when exposure and Justice/Injustice—not safety from the master villain—control the final payoff.
- Choose Horror when dread, helplessness, the unknowable, and confrontation with evil dominate over investigation and the hero’s unique gift.
- Choose Action when kinetic rescue and defeat of the villain dominate over the personalized investigation, false ending, and damnation threat.
- Legal, political, journalism, and financial subject matter does not change the global Thriller value to Justice, Power, or Truth.

## Controlling-Idea Value Rules

- The controlling idea must resolve Life or Damnation.
- Safety is the need/objective; Life is the positive value pole.
- Truth, justice, freedom, sanity, and integrity may enable the Cause or form secondary charges but may not replace the Thriller value.
- The protagonist’s unique gift must be concretely expressed or refused in the climax, not merely recognized.
