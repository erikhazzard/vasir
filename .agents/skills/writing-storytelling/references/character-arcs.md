## Choose what develops

An arc is a specific movement across time, not a requirement that every protagonist acquire a better personality. Identify what the work wants to change, reveal, test, or preserve before selecting a pattern. A person can change strategy, understanding, self-description, moral conduct, capacity, relationships, status, or the audience's interpretation. Several can move differently at once.

| Development | Central question | Evidence the ending needs |
|---|---|---|
| Corrective growth | Can this person revise an inadequate approach? | A changed action when the previous method remains tempting or available. |
| Steadfastness | Can a worthwhile conviction survive new costs and knowledge? | Renewed commitment that responds to the actual new conditions. |
| Corruption | What makes an initially resisted violation increasingly permissible? | A sequence of choices and consequences that changes the person's limits. |
| Disillusionment | What belief becomes untenable, and what remains worth doing? | Lost confidence distinguished from whatever conduct or commitment survives. |
| Delayed insight | What can the person finally understand after repair has become impossible? | Recognition that alters meaning without undoing the established cost. |
| Revelation | What will the work disclose about someone who does not fundamentally change? | New situations or access that revise the audience's account. |
| Expansion of capacity | What can this person now do or receive? | Learned skill, resources, permission, or relationships that make the difference. |
| Erosion or exhaustion | What is lost despite commitment or insight? | The material, bodily, social, or coercive causes of diminishing power. |
| Relational development | What can people now do for, with, or without one another? | A changed practice or arrangement on both sides. |

These can combine. A person can become more capable and more destructive, more merciful and less able to resist domination, or less confident in an institution and more committed to its stated mission. Name the distinct movements rather than averaging them into “positive” or “negative.”

## Track different changes separately

For a substantial arc, sketch only the relevant tracks. A useful minimum is starting belief or method, immediate pursuit, practical capacity, relationship condition, and outcome. Add self-knowledge or moral conduct when the story requires them. Mark what causes each change.

Nera, a flood-threatened archivist, begins with a worthwhile commitment: residents should retain usable evidence of their homes. Her method requires her own supervision of every copy. She can learn that custody must be shared while becoming physically exhausted and losing original documents. Her improved judgment does not reverse the flood or restore every claim.

Track Nera's moral insight and documentary outcome separately. If the ending claims complete success because she finally trusts someone, it has erased material loss. If it calls her a failure because originals are destroyed, it has erased what her changed practice preserves. The ending can give both consequences their full weight.

The same distinction is essential in the LOTR case. Frodo's mercy grows while the Ring increasingly overpowers his capacity to resist it. At the climax he claims the Ring; he does not voluntarily renounce it. Earlier mercy preserves Gollum, whose possessiveness contributes to the Ring's destruction. Do not redescribe Frodo's collapse as a simple moral failure, make moral growth guarantee strength, or give every character his exact trajectory. Use [lotr-case-study.md](lotr-case-study.md) for the fuller interpretive argument.

## Use Bird's corrective progression where it fits

Bird's progression is particularly useful when the plot is an argument with a protagonist's method. Use its causal functions without imposing exact percentages or forcing all stories to begin in dissatisfaction.

1. **Challenge:** An existing problem or aspiration meets an intimidating opportunity and unforeseen opposition. Establish what the person believes can be gained, why the opportunity attracts them, and what they do not yet understand.
2. **Easy way:** They tackle the challenge while protecting the old philosophy. “Easy” describes avoidance of the necessary reconsideration; the attempt may require immense labor or courage. Give the old method real advantages so its persistence is intelligible.
3. **Crash:** Results undermine the plan or the account that sustained it. A catastrophe matters to the arc only if it changes what the person can plausibly believe, attempt, or continue avoiding. Success can perform this function by exposing an intolerable cost.
4. **Hard way:** They engage more directly with the difficulty and begin trying different conduct. Early improvement may be awkward, inadequate, or unrewarded. They can still misunderstand what truly needs to change.
5. **Spiritual crisis:** A further consequence makes competing commitments impossible to reconcile in the old way. The crisis concerns what kind of life or action they will now endorse. It may be quiet, religious, relational, or morally divided; a confession of “my flaw” is not required.
6. **Corrected philosophy tested in action:** The new approach directs a costly, still-difficult act. The act meets reality, and its consequences complete or complicate the correction. The universe does not owe the character a reward for insight.

Bird's own examples include characters who need to stop trying to become someone else. Preserve that possibility. His developmental model also has boundaries: systemic deprivation is not necessarily caused by a victim's philosophy, a rescue story need not correct an initially adequate commitment, and contemplative or communal forms may organize time differently.

## Build a causal arc rather than a lesson schedule

Original developmental sketch, continuous with the archive examples in the character and dialogue references:

**Starting practice.** Nera has repeatedly caught errors that threatened residents' claims. Her exactness and personal responsibility are valuable. She permits Jo to research and draft, but Nera alone can certify extracts. Jo is leaving for a school upriver after the evacuation.

**Opportunity.** Flood preparations create a chance to move the archive and establish a better public service. Nera plans to send complete crates to a safe tower. Iven, the ferrymaster, has limited transport and residents to evacuate. A council clerk offers secure central storage, with access suspended during a lengthy audit. Safety of documents and practical access to rights now diverge.

**Old method wins locally.** Nera personally authenticates a disputed claim and gets a family onto the relocation list. This confirms why people trust her. Other claims wait while she performs the work. The success establishes both the capacity and the bottleneck; it does not need a separate “flaw scene.”

**Crash.** The lower rooms flood before the promised crates can move. The town needs usable extracts rather than intact shelves. Nera's method has helped create delay, but it did not cause the river or every administrative barrier. Keep responsibility proportionate.

**First attempt at change.** Nera trains volunteers to copy. She still insists on checking every page, making herself the same bottleneck under a more generous name. This is plausible partial progress: she has shared labor but retained decision authority. Jo can now name the difference from experience.

**Further consequence.** To finish every check, Nera expects Jo to miss the last passage upriver. Jo refuses. The demand reveals that Nera is preserving the archive by consuming the future it was supposed to protect. Jo is not delivering a lesson prepared for the heroine; Jo is defending an already established independent life.

**Crisis.** Nera can maintain sole authority and preserve only what she can personally verify, or authorize a bounded process that others can complete without her. She cannot both guarantee perfection and make sufficient evidence available in time. Let the hazards of delegation remain credible: errors matter, private petitions must remain protected, and some claims are disputed.

**Action.** She defines what trained witnesses may certify, separates restricted petitions from public extracts, records unresolved discrepancies, and gives Jo authority to proceed without her countersignature. Precision survives as a shared procedure. She loses the satisfaction of personally knowing every result and must let some decisions stand without intervening.

**Outcome.** Several claims survive through copies distributed among residents, the receiving school, and the town clerk. Some originals are lost. Jo leaves. The first later inquiry reaches someone other than Nera, and she lets that person answer. The final action establishes a changed relation to authority after the immediate emergency; it does not certify that she will never struggle with control again.

Each event alters the conditions of the next. Character, plot, and dialogue are descriptions of the same causal sequence: a belief produces a request, another person answers, the answer changes what is possible, and the next choice revises or reasserts the belief. Do not outline a complete plot, then attach introspective “arc moments” whose removal changes nothing.

## Make recognition and action different achievements

Self-knowledge can precede change, follow it, remain partial, or become another form of avoidance. A person may accurately describe their problem while arranging life to continue it. “I know I interfere” can function as an apology, warning, excuse, or demand that others accommodate interference.

Weak turning point:

> “You're right,” Nera said. “I have to trust people.” She felt her fear release.

More consequential version:

> “You can sign the ordinary claims,” Nera said.
>
> “Without bringing them back to you?”
>
> “Put a mark beside anything disputed.”
>
> “That wasn't my question.”
>
> Nera set her seal beside Jo's hand. “Without bringing them back.”

Jo's follow-up distinguishes shared labor from delegated authority. Nera's physical act creates a practical option; the final sentence accepts its terms. Whether this is durable trust still depends on what she does when Jo makes a decision she dislikes.

A costly test needs a live alternative, not merely an announced sacrifice. If Nera cannot reach the papers, allowing Jo to carry them does not by itself establish chosen relinquishment. Make the old option available at a real cost, or describe the result more modestly. If the intended arc concerns exhaustion, an involuntary loss of control may be exactly the right event; just do not relabel it consent.

The moral quality of the new action and its effectiveness are separate questions. A confession may be responsible and still fail to restore trust. A refusal may preserve integrity and cost a job. A villain may learn cooperation to become more dangerous. Preserve causal enabling conditions so success is earned by what actually makes it possible, including help, resources, luck, and other people's choices.

## Design steadfast and adverse development

For steadfastness, test the conviction through new knowledge rather than merely escalating pain. A ferrymaster who believes passengers take priority need not learn to care. They may learn that saving documents also prevents future displacement and revise the means of protecting people. Their original commitment survives while becoming more exact. A stable detective can change the cast's understanding, expose a social arrangement, or acquire a new cost without converting morally.

For corruption, identify the first boundary the character crosses, the reason it seems exceptional, the benefit it produces, the cost transferred elsewhere, and the next permission that follows. Do not treat one sudden evil act as a complete deterioration. A common mechanism is successful rationalization: a violation works, others benefit, and refusing repetition becomes harder. Another is institutional reinforcement: advancement rewards the very conduct the person once resisted. Give the character alternatives in proportion to the responsibility the story assigns.

For disillusionment, specify the belief lost. “People are bad” is usually less useful than “professional merit will be recognized without political bargaining.” A character may lose institutional faith while retaining skill, solidarity, or purpose. Show what action survives the loss and what it now costs.

For delayed insight, let recognition arrive after an irreversible consequence. It can change the final relationship, testimony, or audience judgment without redeeming the damage. Avoid automatically killing the character at the instant of insight; living with changed knowledge may be the more demanding ending.

For tragic steadfastness, preserve the genuine worth of the chosen commitment and the genuine good sacrificed to it. A tragic dilemma weakens if the story later reveals that the rejected good was worthless anyway. An ending may judge a priority, mourn it, remain divided about it, or distinguish what the individual secured from what others lost.

## Allow collective and relational development

Sometimes the key change is who has knowledge, authority, access, or permission. A group learns to coordinate; a caregiver becomes less indispensable; two rivals discover a limited undertaking they can share; a child becomes able to refuse a parent's help without losing the relationship. These developments need observable changes in practice, not only warmer feelings.

Track who contributes what to a climax. Observation, interpretation, decision, authorization, execution, and reception can belong to different people. The protagonist's final gesture may activate earlier work rather than personally accomplish everything. Do not erase that distribution by summarizing the ending as “she learns to believe in herself.”

Relationships can change asynchronously. Nera may sincerely accept Jo's departure before Jo trusts the acceptance. Jo may forgive an interference without staying. Gratitude does not entitle a benefactor to renewed intimacy. The altered arrangement can be meaningful while the participants disagree about what it means.

## Preserve history in a series

Retain learning unless the form explicitly promises reset. New episodes can test a retained principle under new conditions, reveal a competing value, change the people affected, or expose an unrecognized limit. Do not repeatedly make a character forget the same insight simply to reproduce a familiar plot.

The same capacity can generate fresh trouble without cancelling growth. Nera no longer checks every copy; now a dispute arises between two authorized witnesses. She must decide how to review a disagreement without reclaiming all authority. The new difficulty grows from the successful change.

In deliberate reset comedy, recurring dispositions can remain stable because pleasure lies in their permutations and collisions. Track the intended contract: which consequences accumulate, which return to baseline, and whether emotional promises require memory even when practical arrangements reset.

## Revision tests and delivery

An arc sketch should identify the starting conduct, causal pressure, meaningful alternatives, changed or reaffirmed act, actual outcome, and cost that remains. For a requested draft, write the scene that supplies missing evidence rather than returning only the sketch.

Use these tests selectively:

- Remove the mentor's explanation. Is the development still inferable from choices and consequences?
- Give the protagonist the old shortcut again. Does their response establish what the ending claims?
- Keep the new belief but remove a resource or ally. Does the result still happen implausibly?
- Keep the external outcome and reverse the internal one. Can you describe the difference precisely?
- Recenter on the person paying for the protagonist's growth. Does the story still acknowledge their future?
- Compare the final voice with the opening voice. Has the person developed or been replaced by the author's preferred speaker?

Do not score every life for moral improvement. Judge whether the chosen development is earned and whether the ending claims more than the represented acts establish.
