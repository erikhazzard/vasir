# Creating characters who can generate a story

Use this reference to invent one character, deepen an existing person, design an introduction, or diagnose why a protagonist feels generic, inert, incoherent, or difficult to follow. For a relationship or ensemble, continue to [relationships-and-cast.md](relationships-and-cast.md); for development across time, [character-arcs.md](character-arcs.md); for actual speech, [dialogue.md](dialogue.md). Do not turn a request for one usable character into a complete novel outline.

## Start with the requested encounter

Determine the character's job in this work and the experience the user wants: compelling company, a protagonist able to sustain pursuit, a frightening antagonist, a romantic partner, an unreliable narrator, a recurring comic presence, or a briefly encountered NPC. These invite different amounts and kinds of development. A courier who appears for one page may need a task, an attitude toward interruption, and one memorable response. An intimate narrator may need an entire evolving relationship with the reader.

When the brief is open, invent a promising person in an active situation before filling a dossier. Show enough for the writer to hear the voice and imagine the next choice. Use a short scene to discover what profile questions still matter. If the user supplies an established character, distinguish textual evidence from your proposed additions; do not silently rewrite history, motives, identity, or relationships.

A useful character concept contains a particular attraction and a source of consequential response. The attraction might be attention, appetite, humor, precision, tenderness, charisma, opacity, or some difficult combination. Consequential response need not mean dominance or success. Someone can sustain a story through observation, endurance, refusal, interpretation, companionship, or an inability the work asks us to experience.

## Keep the underlying questions distinct

Use the following distinctions where they change a design decision. They are working tools, not required sections of every output.

| Question | Example: Nera, custodian of a river town's archive | Common confusion |
|---|---|---|
| What does this person value? | Residents retaining a recognized place in the town. | A valued good is not yet an actionable objective. |
| What object of desire are they pursuing? | Preserve usable evidence of residents' land claims before the flood. | The object is not the whole meaning of the story. |
| What satisfaction do they expect from obtaining it? | To know that people cannot be erased while she is responsible. | This deeper desire need not be a hidden trauma. |
| What philosophy directs their approach? | A record is safe only while someone answerable controls every alteration. | A philosophy predicts decisions; “stubborn” only describes a tendency. |
| What strategy follows? | Personally authenticate and release every copied claim. | A strategy can change without changing the valued good. |
| What do they want in this scene? | Get Iven to reserve ferry space for archive crates. | A scene objective is more immediate than “belonging.” |
| What else do they want to protect? | Iven's respect, residents' privacy, her trainee's confidence. | These background desires shape how she asks. |
| What is their personality? | Exact, dryly playful, easily absorbed by other people's handwriting. | Personality need not disappear when philosophy changes. |
| What value is changing in the scene? | Residents' practical security improves while trust in Nera declines. | A value's charge describes conditions, not a trait or moral score. |

Keep material needs real. A person who needs housing, time, food, authority, or treatment may need those things even after acquiring insight. If using “need” to mean an author-perceived correction, specify what conduct or understanding would change, and whose interpretation it is. A character's self-accusation is evidence about their self-image; it is not automatically the author's final truth.

Separate other kinds of belief as well. A rule commits someone to conduct; a theory explains how the world works; a motto presents an identity; a ritual organizes repeated action; a plan coordinates an intended future. Changing a plan after new information is not necessarily a spiritual conversion. A ritual can remain because it is pleasurable, useful, or shared.

## Give the person an ongoing life

Ask what they would rather be doing if the plot had not arrived. Then dramatize a small portion that earns attention now. Work supplies knowledge, embodied habits, hierarchy, time pressure, pride, boredom, and economic constraint. Friendship supplies a history of mutual adjustments. Food, recreation, reading, craftsmanship, religious practice, collecting, and idleness can supply desire beyond survival. Choose what belongs to this particular person.

Avoid substituting inventories for life. “Likes tea, collects bells, has a scar” supplies labels. A woman quietly exchanges the cracked cup at her friend's place setting, then takes it back when the friend notices, creates a relationship whose meaning is not exhausted by the object. A detail becomes specific through use, timing, selection, and response.

Let some particulars remain pleasurable without becoming clues or symbols. If every childhood toy eventually defeats a villain, the world begins to advertise its construction. Conversely, pages of preferences that change no perception, rhythm, relation, or felt atmosphere can remain inert. The useful test is whether the encounter is poorer without the detail, not whether every detail drives the central plot.

Backstory earns space when it changes present inference. A lost parent is one possibility among apprenticeship, habit, success, inherited obligation, migration, friendship, religion, a mistaken explanation, or years of unremarkable routine. A character can have many causes without being fully explained. Before adding another formative catastrophe, try specifying what a current action costs or what competence made an old strategy successful.

## Build a consequential pattern

Translate adjectives into behavior under particular conditions. “Proud but kind” leaves the next scene undecided. “She offers help before it can be requested, then becomes brittle if the recipient changes the plan” produces both action and a possible response. The pattern can be written as:

> When this kind of situation occurs, this person tends to attempt this action because they expect this result, while trying to protect this other good.

Establish the pattern through variation. One occurrence creates an impression; another in a different relation lets the reader predict. Later, create circumstances in which a familiar response would have a newly visible cost. The character may repeat it, adapt it, refuse it, or discover they cannot carry it out. Each result says something different.

Do not mechanically alternate opposites to simulate unpredictability. An unexpected action needs conditions that make it intelligible afterward, even if its complete motive remains uncertain. Surprise can disclose a limit, competing loyalty, overlooked ability, different knowledge, or change. It need not unveil a secret identity.

Use a small set of contrast situations when a person feels flat: being corrected, receiving an unearned kindness, losing a minor convenience, facing public praise, having power over a dependent person, and encountering a task they simply enjoy. Select the two or three that would reveal different priorities. Repeating lethal danger may confirm courage while teaching us nothing about generosity, pride, or intimacy.

## Make contradictions playable

McKee's character dimensions are sustained contradictions, not a collection of unrelated adjectives. A contradiction can occur between public conduct and acknowledged desire, between two wants, between conduct in different relationships, or between what a person believes and what their actions suggest. First make its two sides observable; then decide whether an explanation is needed.

“Nera trusts strangers but mistrusts Jo” is incomplete. Perhaps she expects nothing from strangers but holds a trainee to professional standards. Perhaps she fears being replaced. Perhaps strangers receive only public records while Jo handles sensitive petitions. Those explanations predict different behavior. Let a stranger request sensitive material, or let Jo perform a task on which Nera's status does not depend. The response discriminates among hypotheses.

Public identity and private self are different accounts with different access. The admired volunteer who privately thinks she helps only for applause may be partly vain, harshly self-critical, or both. Show what she does when applause is unavailable. Do not assume inward negativity is more authentic than outward care. A courteous performance may be an honest discipline through which a person resists cruelty.

Role variation can also be truthful. A mechanic speaks differently to a colleague, customer, and parent because knowledge and relationships differ. Stable character can reside in the pattern of those adjustments. Avoid diagnosing deceit from code-switching, concealed pain from every joke, or malice from every silence.

## Entangle strengths and flaws precisely

Bird's useful question is what the person would fear losing by changing. A capacity that has helped them can make their present method attractive despite its damage. Specify the mechanism before prescribing an arc.

| Mechanism | Nera's precision becomes harmful when… | A relevant change |
|---|---|---|
| Wrong domain | She treats a resident's grief as a discrepancy to reconcile. | Learn when verification is not the present task. |
| Excessive intensity | She checks each extract so often that too few leave before the flood. | Establish sufficient verification and permit completion. |
| Harmful target | She assembles an impeccable file to discredit someone who challenged her. | Stop using the skill to punish dissent. |
| Wrong priority | Documentary completeness always outranks privacy or evacuation. | Choose among goods without pretending one never mattered. |
| Changed conditions | A method that protected one small office cannot serve a dispersed town. | Redesign responsibility and access for the new scale. |

“Learn to relax” repairs none of these reliably. Nor must every flaw secretly be a gift. Cruel conduct can need to stop; an impairment need not confer a compensating superpower. Entanglement explains resistance where it exists. It does not redeem every harmful condition.

A strong design preserves the capacity while making its governance consequential. The climactic action might use Nera's precision to create a reliable delegation procedure instead of allowing only her own signature. Another story might reveal that her exactness was serving domination all along. Choose by the story's actual argument, not by a universal preference for healthy integration.

## Make attachment specific

Bird's Believe, Care, and Invest identify distinct audience commitments. Use them diagnostically when the work asks us to follow a person through a pursuit.

- **Believe:** We accept a particular person inhabiting a consequential world. Evidence includes coherent attention, situated conduct, patterned responses, an ongoing life, and the friction of other people's realities. Belief does not mean trusting the character's testimony.
- **Care:** Their circumstances have a significance we can feel. Prepare what an injury, opportunity, pleasure, or threatened future means to them. Concern does not imply innocence, approval, or agreement.
- **Invest:** In Bird's narrower sense, we place hopes in this person's capacity to address the problem. Show a relevant response: asking the needed question, using an established skill, noticing an opportunity, organizing help, or preserving one available choice. This does not guarantee success or ethical conduct.

If the work instead invites fascination with a voice, admiration for refusal, contemplation, or curiosity about consequences, name that invitation. Do not redefine Invest to mean every reason anyone might continue reading. The framework has useful limits.

Diagnose the missing relation before adding material. More suffering does little when we already care but cannot distinguish this person from another victim. More competence can reduce uncertainty when the protagonist already seems invincible. Another attractive quirk does little when the character never makes a consequential response. Often one revision can improve several commitments because a skill, injury, and choice belong to the same situation.

Audience empathy and moral allegiance can diverge. Let a resourceful protagonist remain resourceful when the harm they cause becomes visible. Let affected people have credible lives rather than appearing solely to administer the author's verdict. A story can invite mixed, changing responses without treating its opening attraction as a mistake.

## Check what the choice can reveal

Pressure reveals priorities only in relation to the alternatives the person can perceive and use. Distinguish desire, knowledge, skill, opportunity, resources, authority, stamina, choice, and effect. A person may choose courageously and fail; may comply under coercion; may produce a good result accidentally; may have considerable competence within an abusive arrangement.

Before interpreting an act as “her true self,” ask:

1. What did she know at the time?
2. What alternatives did she believe were available, and which actually were?
3. What capacities and permissions did each require?
4. Who would bear the cost of refusal or failure?
5. What does the chosen act establish, and what remains undecided?

Nera signing a transfer because she prefers official approval differs from signing because the only alternative strands evacuees. The same visible signature supports different inferences. If the scene should expose her preference for control, give her a workable, understood alternative and show her rejecting it. If the scene concerns coercion, preserve that fact instead of claiming she has freely revealed her values.

Constrained agency can be very small. A person preserves a contradictory receipt, requests a witness, chooses the timing of disclosure, or withholds one name. Establish what that response changes. Also allow stillness to be stillness: an exhausted character need not secretly be plotting to deserve the reader's attention.

## Worked creation and opening

Original premise: a river town must evacuate. Nera keeps the documents that establish residents' claims to their homes. The archive contains public records and private petitions. She wants the claims to survive without exposing the petitions or surrendering access to a distant authority.

Weak profile: “Nera is a brilliant but stubborn archivist with a tragic past. She distrusts people, has a secret, and must learn to let go.” This could generate dozens of interchangeable plots. It does not specify what she protects, why her method has value, or what letting go would cost.

Stronger working conception: Nera has spent twenty years catching small documentary errors that otherwise dispossess people. She loves identifying a clerk from a crooked numeral and quietly celebrates when Jo, her trainee, identifies one first. Her governing mistake is to treat her own continued supervision as the condition of trustworthy work. Jo has earned a place at a school upriver and leaves tomorrow. Nera has congratulated Jo and already written her into next month's rota.

This conception supplies pleasure, competence, contradiction, an independent second life, and a visible way the flaw acts. It does not require a traumatic explanation. Her public support and administrative refusal can be sincere in different moments until one piece of paper makes them collide.

Weak opening:

> Nera was meticulous, as everyone in town knew. She had dedicated her life to protecting the archive after losing everything she loved. When the flood warning arrived, she realized she would have to trust her young assistant.

Revised opening:

> Jo laid two slips on the desk.
>
> “Same house,” Nera said. “Different clerk.”
>
> “Same clerk. Different hand. He broke his wrist that winter.”
>
> Nera bent close enough for her spectacles to touch the paper. Then she pulled open the drawer where she kept the good biscuits.
>
> The warning bell rang. Jo counted its strokes with one finger still on the claim.
>
> “I'll take the lower shelves,” Jo said. “Give me the storeroom key.”
>
> Nera put a biscuit on Jo's application letter. “Start a list. I'll fetch them.”
>
> “There isn't time for a list.”
>
> “Then begin with the oldest.”
>
> Jo looked at the letter. Nera looked where Jo was looking and moved the biscuit onto a saucer.

The revision changes behavior rather than adding descriptive polish. Nera's pleasure in another person's skill makes affection and contradiction available together. The key request tests an actual transfer of authority. Her response preserves supervision when speed requires delegation. The application has a practical existence before it carries thematic meaning. The next event can now grow from this refusal: Jo must wait, improvise, or challenge her, and any of those changes the evacuation.

Do not explain every implication after drafting unless the user asked for analysis. Let the scene carry the character. A different brief could stop at the biscuit and the handwriting discovery, sustaining the pleasure of company without introducing a flood.

## Deliverables and diagnosis

For a new major character, ordinarily deliver a concise concept, the consequential pattern, a few particular traits demonstrated through behavior, a small set of relationships, and a short sample scene. Include optional arc possibilities only if the user wants development. For a minor character, compress to the details that alter the encounter. For revision, identify the exact missing evidence and revise the relevant passage.

Useful diagnostic substitutions:

| Symptom | Investigate | Revision that can matter |
|---|---|---|
| A beautiful biography produces flat scenes. | Does the past change a current decision? | Replace exposition with a choice that makes one history relevant. |
| The character feels inconsistent. | Changed motive, changed information, role variation, or author convenience? | Establish the missing condition; preserve deliberate uncertainty. |
| A sympathetic protagonist remains dull. | Have we learned how this person notices or responds? | Give the present burden a specific practical response or a compelling mode of attention. |
| A clever protagonist feels invincible. | Is competence domain-specific? Are others allowed to answer unexpectedly? | Let skill create obligations or meet a limit that cannot be solved by more of itself. |
| A morally difficult person is accidentally exonerated. | Whose costs have disappeared from view? | Keep the affected person's experience causally present. |
| The ending erases the original personality. | Did revision confuse philosophy with temperament? | Retain a recognizable habit while changing its use. |
| Every flaw is blamed on childhood. | Is present conduct already understandable? | Remove unnecessary explanation and strengthen current incentives, relationships, and choices. |

Lineage: Bird's *The Secrets of Character* supplies the attachment distinctions, ongoing life, voice patterns, and strength/flaw relation; *The Secrets of Story* connects philosophy, personality, and action. McKee's *Character* supplies dimensions and reciprocal cast design. The agency tests and distinctions among entanglement mechanisms are critical synthesis, not diagnostic psychology.
