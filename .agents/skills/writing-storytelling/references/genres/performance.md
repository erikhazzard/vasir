# Performance

Use when expressing skill under consequential judgment organizes the story.

**Value spectrum:** shame / respect. **Core emotion:** triumph. **Core event:** **The Big Performance Scene**, where the protagonist expresses their gift under prepared pressure. Triumph may be achieved, compromised, or denied; applause is not mandatory.

Conscious desires include winning, securing a commission, completing difficult work, or earning recognition. The underlying need is esteem. Distinguish competitive results, demonstrated mastery, others' respect, and self-respect: they can move in opposite directions. A trophy cannot prove fulfillment; losing cannot confer automatic moral victory.

## Expectations that do work

Treat opportunity, reluctance, training, failed strategy, apparent defeat, and decisive performance as functions rather than a fixed sequence:

- Give the opportunity standards and stakes. Establish who can judge, exclude, or reward the protagonist and what creates the power divide.
- Make reluctance concrete: exposure risks humiliation, belonging, livelihood, or an existing identity. Being forced into the spotlight should change a choice, not merely delay willing participation.
- Use a mentor to transmit demanding knowledge and complicate dependence. Support, challenge, or betrayal should alter practice or judgment. Cruelty is not evidence that the mentor's methods caused excellence.
- Show training change an observable capability, tactic, or limitation. A montage of effort cannot explain an unprepared mastery.
- Let the initial strategy fail for a reason. Discovering an evaluator's standards can expose the limits of imitation, compliance, rivalry, or technical perfection. The apparent defeat should make continuing under the old terms untenable.
- Make the big performance an encounter with those pressures. Show the difficult work and its consequential choices, then register both achievement and cost. “Unconditional expression” means committing without guaranteed approval, not performing without limits or consequences.

## Choose the arena and its costs

**Sports** foreground bodily preparation, rules, and endurance. **Business/profession** makes skilled delivery answer to clients, colleagues, or economic survival. **Art** tests vision against execution and recognition. **Performing arts** concentrates these pressures in an exposed act before others.

Win-but-lose and lose-but-win endings require distinct accounts: professional respect may cost a relationship; an unsuccessful audition may establish self-respect. Preserve both achievement and loss.

## Provisional controlling idea

**Self-respect is secured because a pianist finishes her own demanding composition despite knowing that refusing the prescribed piece costs her the audition.** Value: respect; final charge: positive internally, negative competitively; chief cause: committed expression under an accepted cost. Establish the skill and choice beforehand.
