# Action

Use when the pursuit is stopping a destructive force, saving people, or securing or destroying something dangerous. **Value spectrum:** life/death. **Promised emotion:** excitement through consequential risk, movement, and changing opportunities.

The roles are **hero, villain, and victim**: someone risks themselves to protect; someone spends others for their own ends; someone cannot escape unaided. These positions do not require spotless heroes, cowardly victims, or human villains. Nature, environment, or time can supply antagonism.

## Core event and diagnostic expectations

**The Mercy Scene / Hero at the Mercy of the Villain:** the protagonist is weakest while the opposing force holds decisive power. “Mercy” names being in another's power, not practicing forgiveness. The reversal tests an established capacity, tactic, relationship, or resource; prepared help can be decisive. The source permits this event anywhere, not necessarily at the climax.

- Give the setting active pressures: contested territory, blocked routes, instability, or growth versus dominance hierarchies. Show the opponent's advantage through credible appraisal—the function of a “speech in praise of the villain.”
- Establish what the antagonist wants, why this plan suits them, and what makes it plausible, dangerous, and rewarding to them. Discovering that objective should change the hero's response. A MacGuffin earns its place through the choices its possession creates.
- Use deadlines and set pieces to alter available tactics. Approach, escape, rescue, a scramble for the object, and direct confrontation create different movement problems; interchangeable chases do not demonstrate escalation.
- Diagnose the attack or threat, responsibility accepted or evaded, displacement, failed initial strategy, apparent exhaustion of options, and decisive reversal. Refusal, lashing out, and an “all is lost” moment are possible pressure responses, not compulsory personality traits. Track the cost of sacrifice in the aftermath; reward is not automatic.

## Select the governing constraint

- **Adventure / person against nature:** labyrinth constrains routes; monster supplies a living threat; environment makes the setting dangerous; doomsday makes the environment itself a victim.
- **Duel / person against person:** revenge means hero pursuing villain; hunted reverses pursuit; Machiavellian pits villains against each other; collision has a villain turn protagonists against each other.
- **Epic / person against the state:** rebellion faces a visible tyrant; conspiracy an invisible one; vigilante a criminal organization; savior a force seeking social destruction.
- **Clock / person against time:** ransom uses an imposed deadline; countdown a circumstantial one; holdout requires surviving until help arrives; fate makes time itself the opposition.

**Provisional controlling idea:** Life is preserved because the trapped rescuer abandons a failed solo assault and opens the service passage for the rescue team. This proposes a positive final charge and a concrete enabling cause, not moral improvement as a substitute for tactics.
