# Love

Use when a developing relationship and its future organize the story.

**Value spectrum:** love / hate, or intimacy / estrangement. **Core emotion:** connection and romance. **Core event:** **Proof of Love**—what one person will risk or relinquish for another without guaranteed reciprocation. Its scale comes from the personal cost, not the spectacle.

Conscious desires include affection, mutual understanding, commitment, and a shared future. Both lovers need their own wants and obligations beyond securing the match. A rival may be another person, an incompatible ethic, or a competing commitment; confidants, helpers, and harmers activate social pressures.

## Expectations that do work

Expect meeting or renewed recognition, intimate connection, confession, threatened separation, proof, and a final relational choice. These functions may merge, recur, or change order. An established couple needs no invented first meeting or arbitrary breakup.

- Enact attraction through encounters and the particular pleasure and difficulty of each other's company, not a desirable-trait list. Shared rituals, private language, and changed habits establish intimacy beyond declarations.
- Give opposition a consequential basis: family obligations, social prohibitions, incompatible goals, harmful habits, or beliefs about dependence. An avoidable misunderstanding cannot carry the whole relationship unless inability to ask or answer is itself the developed problem.
- Locate a secret's boundary: society withholding from the couple, the couple from society, lovers from each other, or a lover from themselves. Disclosure should change what can be chosen, not merely postpone an available conversation.
- Contrasting approaches to love can become complementary; show the negotiated change instead of assuming opposites automatically complete each other.
- Make proof respect the beloved's agency. Sacrifice buys no entitlement to affection. Reconciliation, refusal, or separation should follow the relationship's actual choices and consequences.

## Choose the ending promise

**Romantic comedy** uses comic friction to develop a credible shared life. **Tragic love** can establish love while ending its future; happiness is not compulsory. When offered as genre romance, preserve the work's promised hopeful relational outcome.

**Obsession** holds desire apart from mutual recognition and may bring life/death stakes. **Historical** and **paranormal** variants should make their social rules or supernatural differences alter relational choices. **Erotic romance** makes physical intimacy consequential to emotional intimacy. None establishes that someone unable or unwilling to love has a moral defect.

## Provisional controlling idea

**Intimacy is destroyed because a lover secretly sabotages the beloved's scholarship rather than risk separation.** Value: intimacy; final charge: negative; chief cause: possessive interference with the beloved's freedom. Discovery must change the relationship.
