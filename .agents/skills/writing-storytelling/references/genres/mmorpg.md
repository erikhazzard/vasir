# MMORPG: earned power in a persistent world

Build an ordinary person's gradual ascent into capability and a recognized place. The world already has history, work, dangers, and powerful inhabitants. Its unfinished business creates opportunities the player becomes able to pursue.

**Core value:** powerlessness / earned power. **Separate social value:** anonymity / standing. Strength, wealth, knowledge, and reputation can grow differently. **Desired emotions:** aspiration, discovery, pride, and attachment. **Core event for an arc:** the player accomplishes something previously beyond their reach, using gains the journey actually supplied. **Desire:** a next capability, possession, destination, or role worth working toward.

## Make the climb worth inhabiting

- **Begin small and vulnerable.** Food, equipment, a room, a safe route, and one person's trust can matter. Give early work a particular place and beneficiary. Modest stakes still need danger, choice, discovery, or satisfying execution. Delivering supplies can introduce a threatening road and a future ally; an interchangeable errand teaches little about either.
- **Let power change experience.** Levels, spells, equipment, wealth, and practiced tactics should expand what the player can attempt. Revisit a once-dangerous route to make growth tangible. Avoid scaling every familiar threat upward until advancement changes only the displayed numbers.
- **Give the treadmill desirable steps.** Let visible veteran gear and abilities reveal attainable ambitions. Give players time to use each gain. Recurring expeditions, markets, or festivals can expose growth: a porter returns as a trusted guide or supplier. Familiar events should offer new decisions as capability and standing change.
- **Earn the widening scope.** Accumulated deeds can lead from local problems to regional responsibility and eventual world-scale conflict. Overlap arcs of different lengths; let each resolve while other interests continue. Give each new arc understandable immediate stakes. Preserve satisfying stopping points and local lives after epic victories. Players need not converge on the same ruler, raider, or savior role.

## Keep the world larger than its protagonist

- Show places, threats, occupations, and histories beyond the current quest. Geography, travel, preparation, and retreat should affect decisions. The veteran outgrows some dangers while encountering unfamiliar ones; the novice can glimpse a future they cannot yet reach. A journey, rescue, trade, or remembered rivalry can carry a story without a quest marker or cutscene.
- Give factions, settlements, and players independent purposes. Keep recognizable places, relationships, and world rules as other elements change. Ground regional problems in trade, politics, or environment; let solutions create different follow-on situations. The world continues during absence; earlier accomplishments should still count on return.
- Make local impact observable: a usable ferry, a customer's repeat business, trusted access, a guild's remembered rescue. Name who shares the change and how long it lasts. A private quest completion cannot establish a server-wide victory; repeatable content needs an honest relationship to persistent consequences.
- Let recurring encounters create recognition: a reliable healer, known guide, sought-after craftsperson, familiar rival. Trade, danger, complementary abilities, and shared places give people reasons to meet again. Preserve useful independent activity between group expeditions. Cooperation is one route to power and belonging, not the entire fantasy.

## Calibrate the experience

An unknown traveler earns equipment carrying supplies between farms. The wooded road is dangerous. Later, better gear and learned routes make that journey routine. With two familiar players, they restore the valley's mill: a settlement gains flour, and the participants gain customers, access, and recognition. Define that settlement's shared state; later arrivals can join its continuing life. The mountain pass beyond it remains forbidding.

**Provisional controlling idea:** A powerless outsider gains lasting influence because accumulated experience supplies the skills, resources, and relationships needed to pursue greater ambitions. The enacted gains must support that positive final charge. Other arcs may test independence, exploitation, or costly ambition.

Reject instant chosen-one status, world-saving errands before local attachment, ceremonial praise unsupported by changed capability, and content resets that erase every achievement. Ask what the player can now do, who has reason to remember it, and what still lies beyond them.
