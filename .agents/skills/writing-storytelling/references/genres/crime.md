# Crime

Use when criminal wrongdoing, its exposure, and the possibility of justice organize the story.

**Value spectrum:** justice / injustice. **Core emotion:** intrigue and curiosity; satisfaction when evidence connects. **Core event:** **Exposure of the Criminal**. Exposure can succeed while punishment fails; knowledge, admissible proof, and justice are separate achievements.

Conscious desires include identification, justice, exoneration, or committing a successful crime. Investigator, criminal, victim, and suspects establish different stakes and information positions. Retain those harmed by apparently victimless wrongdoing; their loss gives the puzzle consequence.

## Expectations that do work

Treat these as dramatic functions, not a required scene count or chronology:

- Establish the crime and its damage. Make the criminal's desired object intelligible: money, silence, revenge, access. The source calls this the **MacGuffin**; use it to explain conduct.
- Demonstrate the opponent's ingenuity through an effective move or credible assessment. The investigation itself can establish the difficulty without a speech praising villainy.
- Let pursuing clues change the live explanation and available action. Red herrings need credible origins and an eventual intelligible status. Separate fact, testimony, inference, confidence, and proof. Prepare the particular inference the solution needs; merely mentioning a possible mechanism is insufficient.
- Use personal entanglement, manipulation, or a deadline when it changes investigative choices. Increasing urgency should impose a cost on verification, protection, or pursuit.
- Deliver exposure and then establish whether justice follows. In an open crime story, the reader may know the culprit early; the decisive work becomes proving responsibility or making exposure matter. A confession alone need not settle either task.

## Choose the operative subgenre

- **Master detective / cozy:** foreground inference; cozy adds community knowledge and restrained depiction of violence.
- **Historical / paranormal:** establish which investigative methods or powers are available before they resolve the case.
- **Noir / hardboiled:** let corruption complicate what justice can mean or achieve.
- **Police procedural / courtroom:** distinguish investigative discovery from institutional cooperation and admissible proof.
- **Organized crime / caper:** give criminal goals and execution causal priority; do not force a detective to become the protagonist.
- **Newsroom / espionage / prison:** center publication, conflicted loyalties, or institutional confinement as applicable; identification may be only the beginning of redress.

## Provisional controlling idea

**Justice remains defeated because the investigator trades evidence against a powerful employer for her own immunity.** Value: justice; final charge: negative; chief cause: the investigator's self-protective bargain. Enact the exposure and bargain.
