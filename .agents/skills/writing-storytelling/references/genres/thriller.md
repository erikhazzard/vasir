# Thriller

Use when pursuit of safety becomes a contest with a master villain whose crime threatens others and becomes personal. **Value spectrum in this guide:** life/damnation—a fate worse than death. **Promised emotion:** excitement. This engine combines investigation, an active criminal design, and personal jeopardy.

The **protagonist** seeks to stop the villain and prevent further victimization. The **master villain** supplies the threat; the **victim** connects it to wider suffering. The source's “unique gift” is a protagonist-specific capacity. Establish its use and limits; it need not ensure victory.

## Core event and diagnostic expectations

**Hero at the Mercy of the Villain:** usually near the climax, the pursuer becomes vulnerable to the threat they pursued. Make remaining options concrete. An established capacity may enable the response, including prepared help; the outcome does not require flawless solitary execution.

- The **inciting crime** reveals the threat. A “speech in praise of the villain” establishes why overcoming it will be difficult; evidence of planning can carry that function.
- Give the villain an objective or **MacGuffin**, and give the **clock** a consequence. Investigation should affect what can still be prevented before that deadline.
- Use **investigative red herrings** with credible causes and eventual clarification. A wrong lead earns its space by changing exposure, time, or strategy.
- **Making it personal** changes the protagonist's practical situation. The turn in which the protagonist becomes the victim should grow from the conflict.
- A **false ending** reveals danger left unresolved by apparent victory. Prepare its basis and let the final outcome settle the safety question the work actually raised.

## Select the pressure system

- **Serial killer:** recurring, often symbolic crimes organize pursuit.
- **Medical:** conspiracy or outbreak makes care and medical knowledge consequential.
- **Legal:** legal injustice tests professional action with broader consequences.
- **Psychological:** a contest of interpretation and manipulation dominates physical force.
- **Espionage:** international intrigue places national or global safety at stake.
- **Child in jeopardy:** rescuing an endangered child concentrates urgency and vulnerability.
- **Military:** service, conflict, and security threats organize action.
- **Political:** exposing or defeating corruption and conspiracy drives the pursuit.
- **Journalism:** discovering and disclosing suppressed truth creates danger.
- **Financial:** fraud, financial crime, or threatened collapse supplies the mechanism and clock.
- **Woman in jeopardy:** the source names a woman's struggle against personal physical and psychological threat; gender alone does not supply a plot.
- **Hitchcock:** an ordinary person enters extraordinary danger, with suspense foregrounded over action.

**Provisional controlling idea:** Life is preserved because the captive dispatcher turns the killer's rehearsed emergency script into a location code that brings her team before the execution deadline. Her capacity enables others' action; rescue is not an automatic reward for courage.
