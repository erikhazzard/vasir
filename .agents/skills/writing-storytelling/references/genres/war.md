# War

Use when armed conflict tests conduct, allegiance, and sacrifice. A battle setting alone may serve Action; this guide's War model centers fighting's human cost.

## Promise and engine

- **Value spectrum:** honor ↔ dishonor. Establish what the participants call honorable and let conduct and consequences test that account.
- **Promised emotion:** intrigue about how combatants will respond to seemingly impossible conditions.
- **Core event:** **the Big Battle**, testing courage, strategy, and willingness to sacrifice.
- **Desire and roles:** give the viewpoint combatant or unit a concrete mission and an enemy with an intelligible objective. Comrades and commanders create competing claims of kinship, duty, and rank. These roles are inferred from the guide; it supplies no complete role taxonomy.

## Conventions and obligatory functions

The guide favors a broad battlefield or psychological canvas, an ensemble, overwhelming odds, a point of no return, and sacrifice for fellow soldiers. Make pressures specific: what can this unit accomplish, for whom, with which resources? Acceptance of lethal risk can mark commitment without requiring everyone to believe death inevitable.

Find these functions in the chosen structure:

- An attack initiates conflict; reluctance and compelled engagement reveal how position and rank constrain the response.
- Understanding the enemy's purpose changes strategy. Failure of the initial approach demands adaptation with real costs.
- Severe loss tests commitment and kinship; the decisive battle makes tactics and sacrifice consequential.
- Aftermath accounts for what was saved, lost, corrupted, or remembered. The guide's “reward for sacrifice” can mean recognition or enduring bonds; do not invent compensation for an uncompensated loss.

Keep **military outcome, moral outcome, and brotherhood** distinct. A unit can lose ground while preserving comrades or humanity; it can win while betraying them. Survival does not certify honor, and honorable conduct need not secure survival. Neither a commander's conversion nor personal growth is required to explain a tactical result.

## Subgenre emphases

The guide offers controlling-idea sketches, not complete specifications:

- **Pro War:** test the claimed justification for fighting, including what liberation costs.
- **Anti-War:** make the gap between promised glory and devastation consequential.
- **Kinship of War:** center what soldiers owe one another and what survives their sacrifice.

**Provisional controlling idea — illustrative synthesis:** Honor is preserved when a defeated unit risks its escape to evacuate wounded comrades. **Value:** honor. **Final charge:** preserved. **Chief cause:** costly protection of those who cannot protect themselves. Military defeat remains real.
