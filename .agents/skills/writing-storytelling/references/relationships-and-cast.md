## Design relations that produce events

A cast is an arrangement of desires, dependencies, affinities, knowledge, authority, and opportunities. The members reveal one another partly because they cause one another to act. Replace “the mentor represents wisdom” with what this mentor wants from the learner, what the learner can provide or refuse, and what the encounter changes for both.

For a substantial relationship, find a particular shared attraction or history, a present transaction, and a limit. The limit need not be a concealed hostility. Two devoted people can differ about how to spend a future, when help becomes intrusion, or what sacrifice can reasonably be requested. They can also enjoy uncomplicated company. Ordinary pleasure gives a relationship positive substance before anything threatens it.

Avoid distributing one psychological trait to each companion. A protagonist surrounded by Courage, Compassion, Cynicism, and Wisdom has a debating apparatus. Particular people may illuminate those qualities, but they also pursue different goods, use different means, and sometimes surprise the protagonist's account of them. Make their differences matter in action.

## Give the cast different futures

For each recurring consequential person, ask what outcome they would seek if the protagonist disappeared. The answer can be modest: finish a shift, protect a friend, leave town, sell a boat, keep a ritual alive, be allowed to rest. Independence does not require an elaborate subplot or secret ambition. It requires that their responses arise from a life the hero does not own.

Original ensemble: floodwater threatens a town archive containing evidence of residents' land claims and sensitive personal petitions.

| Person | Desired future | Useful capacity or position | What they will not casually surrender |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nera, archive custodian | Usable records remain available to residents. | Knows the historical records and can certify extracts. | Reliability, privacy, and her personal supervision. |
| Jo, trainee | Leave for a school upriver after helping evacuate. | Knows the filing system and can teach volunteers to copy. | An earned future beyond Nera's office. |
| Iven, ferrymaster | Get passengers across before the crossing closes. | Controls loading order and knows practical limits. | Passenger places for an undefined archival obligation. |
| Pell, council clerk | Establish defensible custody after the disaster. | Can arrange dry storage and official recognition. | Accountability for errors and unauthorized disclosure. |
| Seli, resident | Preserve a disputed claim without making a private petition public. | Knows a household history the ledger cannot explain alone. | Privacy as the automatic price of being believed. |

The contrasts generate choices. Pell can be a serious source of opposition without being secretly corrupt: central custody may protect accuracy while delaying the access residents need. Iven's refusal can be responsible rather than callous. Jo's departure can remain a positive future rather than proof of insufficient devotion. Seli can need the archive and resist the custodian who speaks on her behalf.

A coherent cast need not give everyone the same lesson. The story can preserve claims through distributed work while Jo leaves, Pell accepts a limited provisional procedure, and Seli declines to disclose parts of her history. Bring their consequences into meaningful relation; do not force convergence into one morally improved personality.

## Map the relationships that matter

Use a small table or annotated network only when it makes several interacting relations easier to grasp. An edge should say more than “friend,” “enemy,” or “secretly in love.” Track the current arrangement and what can change it.

| Relationship question | Why it changes scenes |
|---|---|
| What can each person ask of the other? | A request may be normal, intimate, presumptuous, or forbidden before its content changes. |
| What does each owe, and who recognizes the debt? | One-sided gratitude can become leverage or resentment. |
| Who knows what, and who knows the other knows? | Private knowledge and public acknowledgment permit different actions. |
| What work or resource passes between them? | Affection alone does not explain who can act. |
| What can each safely refuse? | Apparent cooperation may conceal dependence or coercion. |
| What do they enjoy together? | The relationship acquires value beyond its problem-solving function. |
| What story does each tell about the relationship? | Conflicting accounts can coexist until an event requires one public answer. |
| What changes after this encounter? | The relationship becomes part of plot rather than background description. |

Nera describes Jo as “my successor.” Jo describes Nera as “the person who taught me enough to leave.” They may both be grateful and sincere. A school departure date forces those compatible-sounding accounts into conflict. Their argument need not disclose a lie; it can reveal incompatible assumptions that previously caused no visible collision.

Track only active edges. For a large ensemble, prioritize the relations that exchange consequential information, authority, care, or obstruction in the current section. A complete matrix of every possible pairing becomes expensive bookkeeping unless the work actually uses it.

## Distinguish position from personality

Comparison under shared pressure can make differences legible: one person counts bodies, another preserves evidence, another checks the route, another challenges the official order. But shared circumstances do not mean equal knowledge, power, obligations, or capacity. The quiet person may be the only one holding a child; the apparent coward may know that the boat's second engine is gone.

Before using a common test to establish character, inspect unequal conditions. Then vary those conditions if the distinction matters. Give the quiet person an informed opportunity to speak; give the loud one responsibility for the consequence. The later scene can confirm or revise the first impression.

Institutional role is a source of behavior without being an entire person. A council clerk may care about procedures because previous improvisation harmed residents, because their job depends on compliance, because rules protect against favoritism, or because procedure conceals indecision. Choose the relevant causes from the text and situation. Do not assume the person administering a barrier invented or endorses every part of it.

Bridge characters occupy multiple worlds and can connect information or trust. Their access also produces divided obligations. A translator can keep a negotiation alive while creating different expectations on each side; a veteran can be trusted by residents and officers for incompatible reasons. Define what the bridge cannot know, promise, or carry. Fluent movement between groups is not omniscience.

## Build chemistry and intimacy

Chemistry is partly responsive particularity: one person notices something exact about another and the other responds in a way a stranger would not. It can appear in teasing, curiosity, shared competence, mutual recognition, an unexpected accommodation, or pleasure in someone else's attention. Establish what makes this pair enjoyable or consequential together before claiming that they belong together.

Original quiet encounter:

> Every Thursday, Davi repaired umbrellas beside the café window. Mina waited until he had aligned the crooked handles, then rested hers across all of them.
>
> “Yours has a stand,” he said.
>
> “Mine likes company.”
>
> The next week he had made a stand wide enough for two.
>
> Mina put her umbrella on the table anyway. Then she moved her chair beside his.

The shared behavior permits variation and invitation. Neither character needs to win a contest. The second accommodation changes the relation because earlier repetition has taught the reader what it means. Let the pleasure remain present rather than immediately translating it into a psychological diagnosis.

For a romantic development, distinguish attraction, trust, compatibility, commitment, desire, and capacity to sustain a shared life. These can move at different speeds. A persuasive love scene may increase intimacy while exposing incompatible futures. A declaration does not settle logistics, consent, past injury, or the ability to stay.

Recognition can be a turning point without correction. “I understand you” matters when it names something the recipient has struggled to make perceptible. But recognition is also an action the other person can resist. “You're the strong one” can honor effort or confine someone to unpaid endurance. Show whether the description enlarges or restricts what the recipient can ask for next.

Banter works when the pair can answer. Track whether teasing is returned, welcomed, endured, or used to control the audience. The same joke privately shared can become a public humiliation. Let a character decline the usual game without treating the refusal as proof that they have lost their personality.

## Make conflict reciprocal

Recurring supporting characters should generally leave major encounters with changed possibilities of their own. If Jo exposes Nera's controlling behavior and then vanishes until the next required lesson, Jo remains an instrument. If refusing also changes Jo's passage, professional reference, confidence, or friendship with another volunteer, the event belongs to both lives.

Do not manufacture durable conflict from a clarification that everyone has both reason and freedom to give. When a misunderstanding persists, establish the reason correction is unavailable, disbelieved, costly, mistimed, or insufficient. Sometimes both parties know the same facts and disagree about what they permit. That can sustain a stronger relationship than a secret artificially withheld.

Reconciliation needs a changed practice when the conflict concerned practice. Nera apologizing does not itself remove her countersignature requirement. A parent accepting an adult child may need to stop entering the room without knocking. A repaired friendship might make refusal survivable, not restore identical closeness. Keep restitution, forgiveness, renewed access, and affection distinct.

Asymmetry can be the subject. One person may understand more, love more, hold more authority, or have less freedom to leave. A scene need not artificially balance them, but its interpretation should preserve what the asymmetry changes. Competence or apparent composure does not erase subordination. Gratitude can coexist with anger; care can coexist with domination.

## Work with cast functions and scale

Distinguish protagonist, viewpoint, narrator, agent of decisive action, antagonist, foil, intimate, witness, and service role. One character may occupy several; they need not all belong to the same person. Avoid importing inconsistent craft terminology when ordinary descriptions are clearer.

A group protagonist needs a sufficiently shared undertaking and linked fortunes. A roomful of individually motivated people may instead form multiple protagonists, an ensemble portrait, or intersecting stories. Identify how the audience follows the whole: shared place, central event, transferred object, rotating viewpoint, repeated ritual, or cumulative consequence.

Minor characters do not each need an arc. Give them the degree of specificity necessary for their encounter: a task already underway, a distinctive attention, a permissible refusal, a particular kindness, an independent timetable. The impression of further life often comes from a small resistance to the protagonist's convenience.

When considering a merge, ask whether two characters differ in causal access, ethical position, relationship history, or effect on the protagonist. If they only deliver the same type of information in the same emotional register, merging may concentrate the work. If their different positions make the information mean different things, preserve that distinction. Do not merge merely to reach an arbitrary cast limit.

Nonhuman, mythic, symbolic, and deliberately opaque figures can have relations without a conventional psychological dossier. Establish the form's rules of responsiveness: what attracts, repels, obligates, transforms, or remains unknowable. Do not automatically humanize a spirit into a traumatized contemporary speaker. Route to [world-and-myth.md](world-and-myth.md) when symbolic or cosmological function changes the design.

## Distribute the climax

Identify who observes, interprets, authorizes, prepares, acts, and bears the result. Earlier supporting work should remain causally necessary if the ending claims collective achievement. A heroine who accepts help and then personally performs every meaningful act has accepted the idea more than the arrangement.

In the archive story, Seli identifies a misleading entry, Jo trains the copyists, Pell establishes a provisional certification rule, Iven carries passengers who can take copies with them, and Nera relinquishes exclusive authority while protecting restricted material. This is not a roll call of equal heroism. Their distinct positions make a particular outcome possible.

A person can remain the emotional center while hope for effective action moves elsewhere. Do not call a dependent protagonist defective because others contribute what they cannot. Conversely, a rescuer who arrives with everything needed but no prior causal connection can replace the story's work instead of completing it.

## Worked ensemble revision

Weak design: “Nera's devoted assistant helps her learn to trust. The heartless boat captain obstructs the rescue. The corrupt clerk wants the archive. A grateful resident shows why it matters.” Everyone already agrees with the author's verdict about their function. Most exchanges can only repeat it.

Revision changes an actual scene. Nera offers Jo the future she assumes Jo wants:

> “When this is over,” Nera said, “the office will be yours.”
>
> Jo tied the last bundle. “I sent you my acceptance letter.”
>
> “I know. I meant you wouldn't have to go.”
>
> “I want to go.”
>
> Nera watched the string cut into the damp paper. “After everything we've done here?”
>
> “Because of it.”

Jo's response preserves gratitude while refusing the proposed reward. Nera must answer a person rather than an assigned lesson. If she now asks Jo to stay, the request carries a known cost. If Jo helps for one more hour, the hour becomes a bounded gift rather than assumed service.

Now let Iven's constraint answer that choice: one hour remains before the crossing closes. Pell can offer storage, but an audit delays access. Seli refuses to expose a petition just to accelerate the claims. Their positions create a concrete design problem for Nera: reliable, privacy-respecting evidence must become usable without complete central custody. The cast has generated a plot solution and a dilemma that no isolated personality sheet could supply.

## Delivery and diagnosis

For an ensemble request, deliver the cast's distinct pursuits, a compact map of consequential relationships, and one collision that makes the differences visible. For a relationship request, focus on its present arrangement, attraction, contested expectation, and next meaningful exchange. For a draft request, produce the exchange itself.

Useful checks: can a supporting person credibly refuse; does the refusal change anything; is a helper's future still present after the hero improves; does affection have a positive texture; do unequal resources change the interpretation of behavior; can the same central good support different legitimate plans; does the climax preserve the contributions it depends on?

Lineage: McKee's *Character* supplies the reciprocal cast principle and distinctions among narrative functions. Bird's character and story books contribute comparative responses, life beyond the plot, friendship, friction, and recognition. Distributed agency, boundaries of help, and asynchronous development are synthesis extensions. See [sources-and-lineage.md](sources-and-lineage.md).
